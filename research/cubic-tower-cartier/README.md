# All 240 twelve-dimensional factors are absolutely simple

For each of the 120 three-index supports in the tenth cubic tower stage, **both signed factors are absolutely simple over the algebraic closure of the rationals**. Each has dimension twelve. A single ordinary reduction suffices in 142 cases. Two primes suffice in 97 cases, and one case uses three. The largest witness prime is 167. A second computation gives the complete degree-110 zeta numerator of the genus-55 stage at 11 modulo 33, together with its first eight nonconstant integer coefficients. ## The Cartier matrix on one signed factor

Fix a simultaneous good prime $p>3$ as defined in the tower construction. On the base put $w^2=f(z)=-4z^3+z^2+z+1$. For $i$ in the support $S$ of size $k$, write $P_i(t_i)=t_i^3+3(z+i)t_i-w$, and $P'_i=3(t_i^2+z+i)$. On the smooth affine part, the regular differentials have the form

$$
\omega=\frac{dz}{2w\prod_i P'_i}\sum_e a_e(z,w)\prod_i t_i^{e_i},
\qquad a_e\in\mathbb F_p[z,w]/(w^2-f).
$$

This follows by affine adjunction for the triangular complete intersection. Equivalently each monic separable cubic has trace-dual fractional module generated by $1/P'_i,t_i/P'_i,t_i^2/P'_i$. The branch loci of the different cubics are disjoint over the elliptic base, so their affine fibre product is regular. The traces of $t_i^e/P'_i$ for $e=0,1,2$ are $0,0,1$ by interpolation in the three roots. The full-support condition is vanishing of the trace at every forgotten index. It therefore restricts every exponent $e_i$ to zero or one.

The remaining conditions are regularity over infinity and the sign of the simultaneous negation of $w$ and all $t_i$. At infinity on the elliptic base let $\tau=z/w$ and $q=w/(2z)$. On each normalized triple of sheets, $1/P'_i$ and $(t_i-q)/P'_i$ vanish to order at least one. After division by $\tau$ their leading-value vectors are independent. Indeed the solitary root has $t_i\sim-1/\tau$ and a denominator of order $-2$. The other two roots have $t_i=q\pm b+O(\tau)$ with $b^2=-(12i+1)/12\ne0$ and simple poles in the denominators. The first vector vanishes only on the solitary sheet; the second is nonzero there. Independence can be checked over the algebraic closure. Tensor products of these leading vectors remain independent on the full product of the normalized sheets.

Expand the numerator in the basis of products of $(t_i-q)$ indexed by $j\subseteq S$. The new coefficients are
$$
a'_j=\sum_{e\supseteq j}a_e q^{|e|-|j|}.
$$
Since $dz/(2w)$ is regular and nonvanishing on the elliptic base, the differential is holomorphic exactly when every $a'_j$ has pole order at most $k$ at infinity. Inverting this triangular change gives the bound $2k-|e|$ on the pole order of $a_e$. Write each $a_e$ using monomials $z^a w^b$ with $a\ge0$, $b\in\{0,1\}$, and $2a+3b\le 2k-|e|$. For each $j$ expand $a'_j$ using $w^2=f$ and impose zero on every coefficient of pole order greater than $k$. The Laurent monomials $z^a w^b$ have distinct orders for these two values of $b$, including negative $a$. The sign of the original differential monomial is $(-1)^{b+|e|+1}$. Linear algebra gives $k2^{k-1}$ basis vectors per sign, as in the characteristic-zero projectors. [Differential basis and matrices](cartier.py)

Let $F_0=w^2-f(z)$ and let $N$ denote a numerator. To apply Cartier, form
$$
N\bigl(F_0\prod_i P_i\bigr)^{p-1}.
$$
In **each** of $z,w,t_i$, select exponents $mp+p-1$ and replace them by $m$. The resulting polynomial is the new numerator after restriction to the curve. For a direct proof, write the differential as the iterated residue of the ambient top differential divided by $F_0\prod_iP_i$. Move the $p$-th powers of these denominators through Cartier. Cartier on a polynomial top differential in affine coordinates has exactly the indicated monomial rule. It commutes with the iterated residues: in the local coordinate system $z,F_0,(P_i)_i$ at a generic unramified point, selecting the logarithmic coefficient with exponent $-1$ commutes with selecting the exponents congruent to $p-1$. Equality on this dense open gives equality of the resulting rational differentials.

The implementation extracts the $t_i$ coordinates first. For input exponent $e_i$ and output exponent $h_i$, these are the multinomial terms with $3a+b=(h_i+1)p-1-e_i$ and $a+b+c=p-1$. Their contribution before extraction in $w,z$ is
$$
 {p-1\choose a,b,c}3^b(z+i)^b(-w)^c.
$$
Reduce their product times the source numerator modulo $w^2-f$. This reduction preserves the resulting differential since adding a multiple of $F_0$ before multiplying by $F_0^{p-1}$ changes the output by a multiple of $F_0$. For the reduced expression $A(z)+wB(z)$, extraction in $w$ yields the pair $f^{(p-1)/2}A,B$. Extraction in $z$ then completes the computation. The verifier tests preservation of the holomorphic linear conditions and reproduces every coefficient modulo 41 of the 110 previously known singleton and pair polynomials. It also compares all 3,120 coefficients of the 240 triple-support numerators at 41 with an independent finite-fibre calculation.

The relative Frobenius on $H^1(\mathcal O)$ is dual to Cartier on holomorphic differentials. The Frobenius numerator modulo $p$ is $\det(1-TC)$ for the Cartier matrix $C$ over the prime field. The same congruence holds on these signed pieces because the finite pullback, trace and involution maps commute with the cohomological operations, and the denominators of the projectors are powers of two and three. Equivalently one can apply the trace congruence to the quotients by the sign involution on every subsupport, then divide the zeta factors successively. A nonzero determinant gives ordinary reduction of this factor. ## A dimension restriction valid over every finite extension

Let $B$ be a signed factor of dimension $g$ with ordinary reduction at $p$. Over the $p$-adics its $2g$ characteristic-zero Frobenius roots split into $g$ units and $g$ roots of valuation one, with multiplicity. Write $H(X)$ for the degree-$g$ characteristic polynomial of $C$. Its roots give the residues of the unit roots. Put
$$
 M=\operatorname{lcm}\{n:\varphi(n)\le (2g)^2\}.
$$
Every root-of-unity quotient of two characteristic-zero Frobenius roots has order dividing $M$ because its field has degree at most $(2g)^2$. The program constructs $M$ by taking, for each prime $\ell\le (2g)^2+1$, the largest $\ell^a$ with $\varphi(\ell^a)\le(2g)^2$.

An abelian subvariety of dimension $d$ over the geometric special fibre is defined over some extension of degree $r$. Every subvariety of an ordinary abelian variety is ordinary. Its unit roots, counted with multiplicity, therefore select $d$ eigenvalues of $F^{rM}$ in a multiset stable under the local Galois group at $p$. The assignment $\alpha^{rM}\mapsto \alpha^M$ is well-defined because equality of the former powers implies the original quotient is a root of unity of order dividing $M$. This assignment respects the Galois action. Reducing the selected powers $\alpha^M$ modulo $p$ gives a Frobenius-stable submultiset of the roots of $H$ raised to $M$. Consequently $d$ must be a sum of degrees of irreducible factors of this powered polynomial, with no multiplicity exceeded. The test allows repeated roots at every step. Considering $1\le d\le\lfloor g/2\rfloor$ suffices by the existence of an isogeny complement.

To calculate those degrees, take each irreducible $h\mid H$ of degree $n$ and multiplicity $m$. The degree of a root raised to $M$ is the least $s\mid n$ for which $X^{M(p^s-1)}=1\bmod h$. The exponent may be reduced modulo $p^n-1$. This contributes $mn/s$ factors of degree $s$, and a finite subset-sum gives all permitted dimensions. A characteristic-zero geometric abelian subvariety specializes as an abelian subvariety of the same dimension at every good prime, after finite extension. Empty intersection of the permitted dimensions thus proves absolute simplicity. All 240 intersections are empty. In particular the positive $(0,1,2)$ factor already has no possible proper dimension at 17. Its degree-12 unit polynomial, in descending order, has coefficients $1,5,7,1,11,7,16,12,16,7,7,5,12$ modulo 17. [Dimension verifier](geometric_dimensions.py)

## Higher precision after base change

Over the degree-33 extension of $\mathbb F_{11}$, the full genus-55 numerator and both top signed numerators are determined coefficientwise modulo $1089=33^2$. This is an extension-field statement. The base-field numerators are known modulo 33 in full and over the integers to the orders above. More generally, if $P(T)=\det(1-TF)$ is known modulo $m$, then the degree-$r$ extension yields $\det(1-TF^r)$ modulo $m\prod_{p\mid m}p^{v_p(r)}$.

Here is the elementary precision argument. Two integral companion matrices for different coefficient lifts agree modulo $p^a$ if their characteristic polynomials agree modulo $p^a$. Their exterior powers also agree modulo $p^a$. It suffices to compare traces of $r$th powers of matrices congruent modulo $p^a$, applied to these exterior powers. First write $r=up^k$ with $p\nmid u$, and take the $u$th powers first. Write the resulting matrices as $A$ and $A+p^a D$. Expand the trace of the $p^k$th power in words in $A$ and $D$. A cyclic orbit of words containing at least one $D$ and having length $p^s$ contributes a multiple of $p^{s+a p^{k-s}}$, since the word repeats $p^{k-s}$ times and cyclic rotations have the same trace. This exponent is at least $a+k$. Thus every coefficient gains the claimed precision. The program applies Newton identities to an integral lift at enough working precision to divide exactly by the required indices. Synthetic polynomials with explicitly known roots, including repeated residue roots, independently check the congruence. ## Replay

With Python 3.11 or later, NumPy and SymPy, run `python verify.py`. This rebuilds the 311 supporting matrix pairs, the 110 lower-support comparisons, the 240 independent triple comparisons at 41, all dimension restrictions, the Newton coefficients and the complete modulo-33 polynomials. To repeat the witness search run `python geometric_dimensions.py --search`. To independently regenerate the large exact counts, compile the C++17 counter and supply the extension degree. The degree-eight computation uses approximately 2.2 GB for its field and histogram tables. The standard Python replay uses the recorded exact counts, and checks them against the two congruences.

```sh
c++ -O3 -std=c++17 extensions.cpp -o extensions
./extensions 7
./extensions 8
```
