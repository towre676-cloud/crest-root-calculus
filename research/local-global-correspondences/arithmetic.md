# The complete geometric and rational isogeny spectra

Let $A$ be the Jacobian of a smooth genus-two curve over $\mathbb Q$, with canonical principal polarization $\lambda_A$. We distinguish isogenies up to target isomorphism. The targets here are Jacobians with their canonical principal polarizations. Kernels and isogenies are geometric unless a field is specified.

## Frobenius upper bounds for Hom

Count the source and target over $\mathbb F_p$ and $\mathbb F_{p^2}$ at a good odd prime. For a binary sextic with leading coefficient $f_6$, the count is $q+1+\sum_{x\in\mathbb F_q}\chi(f(x))+\chi(f_6)$, which includes the normalized infinity fibre. The two counts give the full Weil characteristic polynomial $P_p(t)=t^4+a t^3+b t^2+pa t+p^2$. If a geometric homomorphism is nonzero, it specializes after a finite extension. Thus some source and target Frobenius eigenvalues have ratio a root of unity. Conversely, absence of a cyclotomic factor in their cross-root resultant proves geometric Hom vanishing. Absence of the ratio 1 excludes rational Hom at that prime.

For a geometric endomorphism upper bound, require $P_p$ irreducible and only the diagonal roots in its self-ratio resultant to be roots of unity. Every power of Frobenius then has four distinct eigenvalues on the Tate space. Its centralizer has dimension four. Each endomorphism over the algebraic closure is defined over a finite extension, while the field $K_p=\mathbb Q(\pi_p)$ of degree four already acts. Thus the geometric endomorphism algebra of that reduction equals $K_p$. This uses the faithful Tate-module action. The quadratic field of $\pi_p+p/\pi_p$ has squareclass $a^2-4(b-2p)$. A nonsquare discriminant of the irreducible quartic excludes a biquadratic field, as its regular Klein-four Galois group consists of even permutations. Thus the real quadratic subfield is unique. If two primes give different such fields, the common characteristic-zero algebra that embeds into both is $\mathbb Q$. The integral endomorphism ring is $\mathbb Z$. [Frobenius and field witnesses](frobenius.py) · [Lombardo, geometric endomorphism rings](https://doi.org/10.1090/mcom/3358)

Once $\operatorname{End}_{\overline{\mathbb Q}}A=\mathbb Z$, any exhibited isogeny $A\to B$ makes $\operatorname{Hom}(A,B)$ a rank-one lattice. A degree-four generator candidate cannot divide by an integer $n>1$, because multiplication by $n$ on an abelian surface has degree $n^4\ge16$. Its multiples have exactly the degrees $4n^4$. A quadratic twist multiplies its action under Galois by the twisting character. The nonzero differential matrix therefore determines the generator's quadratic field exactly.

## Counting all geometric targets

Every isogenous surface also has geometric endomorphism algebra $\mathbb Q$. Pulling back a principal polarization gives $\phi^*\lambda_B=m\lambda_A$ for a positive integer $m$, since the symmetric endomorphisms of $A$ are the integers. In dimension two this forces $\deg\phi=m^2$. Conversely a self-dual isotropic subgroup $H$ of $A[m]$ of order $m^2$ admits the unique descended principal polarization on $A/H$. An absolutely simple principally polarized surface in characteristic zero is the Jacobian of a smooth genus-two curve. For a given $m$ distinct kernels give different geometric target isomorphism classes by the rank-one Hom argument. [Polarization descent and principally polarized surface quotients](https://people.maths.ox.ac.uk/~flynn/arts/art39.pdf)

Chinese remaindering separates the primes in $m$. Fix $m=\ell^r$, and equip $M=(\mathbb Z/\ell^r)^4$ with its perfect symplectic pairing. Let $s$ be the dimension of the image of $H$ in $M/\ell M$. This is isotropic, so $s\le2$. If $s=0$, then $H\subset\ell M$, equivalently $H\supset (\ell M)^\perp=A[\ell]$ by self-duality. Such an isogeny factors through multiplication by $\ell$. Removing that scalar gives exactly the multiplier-$\ell^{r-2}$ kernels. For $r=1$ this case is impossible.

For $s=2$, two independent mod-$\ell$ generators span a free Lagrangian summand, already of the required order. Over the residue field there are $(\ell+1)(\ell^2+1)$ Lagrangian planes, by counting incident lines and planes in a symplectic four-space. Each Lagrangian lifts by exactly $\ell^3$ at each successive level, since the lift graphs are parametrized by symmetric two-by-two matrices over the residue field.

For $s=1$, count pairs $(v,H)$ with $v$ primitive in the ambient module and contained in $H$. Each $H$ has $\ell^{2r}(1-1/\ell)$ such vectors. The ambient module has $\ell^{4r}-\ell^{4r-4}$. For a fixed $v$, the symplectic quotient $v^\perp/\langle v\rangle$ is free of rank two. The image of $H$ in this quotient has zero mod-$\ell$ image, so division by the nonprimitive layer leaves a self-dual subgroup at level $r-2$. Its count is $1+\ell+\cdots+\ell^{r-2}$. In rank two these are all index-$\ell^{r-2}$ sublattices, counted by triangular Hermite forms. This gives the second summand in the [stated formula](README.md#all-geometric-quotient-fields-and-all-degrees). For $r=1$ there is no one-dimensional Lagrangian.

The independent four-dimensional enumeration lists upper-triangular Hermite bases of determinant $m^2$ and imposes the six symplectic pairings modulo $m$. Such an integral lattice $L$ satisfies $L\subseteq m L^\#$, and equality follows by equal determinant, so it indeed contains $m\mathbb Z^4$. A common prime divisor of every matrix entry and $m$ detects a nonprimitive kernel. The direct counts agree for multipliers 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8 and 9. ## A finite test covering every odd prime

A primitive rational isogeny with an odd factor $\ell$ in its multiplier gives a nonzero Galois-stable isotropic space $W\subset A[\ell]$ of dimension one or two by the reduction above. For $\ell>3$ of good or semistable reduction, the determinant of an absolutely irreducible subquotient of dimension $d$ is $\epsilon\chi_\ell^j$ with $0\le j\le d$ and $\epsilon^{120}=1$. Here $\chi_\ell$ is the cyclotomic character. The same determinant bound follows for a reducible space by multiplication of its one-dimensional factors. We use the following polarized special case to bound the primes without computing modular-form spaces. [Banwait, Brumer, Kim, Klagsbrun, Mayle, Srinivasan and Vogt, Corollary 2.8](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.02222)

For an auxiliary good $p\ne\ell$, put $q=p^{120}$ and write the characteristic polynomial of $F_p^{120}$ as $P=t^4+a t^3+b t^2+qa t+q^2$.
   
| Galois subspace over $\overline{\mathbb F}_\ell$ | Integer divisible by $\ell$ |
|---|---|
| A stable line in $W$ | $P(1)$ |
| $W$ is a Lagrangian plane with determinant exponent $j=0$ or 2 | $\det(1-\bigwedge^2 F_p^{120})$ |
| $W$ is a Lagrangian plane with determinant exponent $j=1$ | $a^2-4(b-2q)$ |

For the line, an eigenvalue after the 120th power is 1 or $q$. Reciprocal pairing also gives 1 in the second case. For the plane, the quotient is $W^*\otimes\chi_\ell$. If $j=0$ or 2, its determinant or the plane's determinant is 1 after powering. If $j=1$, the determinants on $W$ and on its quotient are both $q$ at this powered Frobenius. A two-dimensional representation satisfies $W^*\otimes\det W\simeq W$, so their characteristic polynomials at this element agree. Consequently $P=(t^2+(a/2)t+q)^2$ modulo $\ell$. These arguments use only characteristic polynomials and apply to nonsplit extensions as well.

Take the gcd in each column of tests over auxiliary primes, multiplying each integer by $p$ first to allow the prime $\ell=p$. A putative $W$ has one fixed type and therefore its prime divides one of the three gcds. Include the bad-model primes and 3 independently. At each remaining odd candidate a good prime with irreducible degree-four Frobenius modulo $\ell$ excludes $W$. The program computes the powers by Newton recurrence. The replay independently raises a companion matrix to the 120th power and calculates the six-dimensional exterior-square determinant from its minors.

On the first source the three gcds are $1324781568$, $1310979734179213981815668736000$ and $88899080284422144$. Including the bad-model primes leaves exactly $3,5,7,11,13,23,31,41,61,223$. Every one has an irreducibility witness. The catalog source leaves eleven odd candidates, all excluded as well. This proves the absence of primitive rational isogenies at every odd prime, including primes arbitrarily larger than the auxiliary witnesses. The separate finite test below ten million uses the following criterion. The polynomial $t^4+at^3+bt^2+pat+p^2$ is irreducible at odd $\ell\ne p$ if and only if both $a^2-4(b-2p)$ and $(b+2p)^2-4pa^2$ are nonsquares. This follows by adjoining $t+p/t$, then testing the quadratic discriminant by its norm. [All-prime certificate](all_primes.py) · [Finite cross-check](rational_spectrum.py)

## Rational two-power kernels and rational components

If $A[2]$ has no rational nonzero element, a rational primitive two-power kernel cannot have $s=1$. For $s=2$ the kernel is free of rank two over $\mathbb Z/2^r$ and its characteristic subgroups $H[2^j]$ give a chain of rational Richelot isogenies of length $r$. Every step is a rational edge. If the source has one rational kernel and the dual target also has one, the next step is forced to be the reverse. Composing an edge with its adjoint is multiplication by 2, so an immediate backtrack cannot occur in this primitive free-kernel chain. The identity and the unique Richelot map exhaust the primitive rational maps. Multiplication then gives the two degree series in the [main table](README.md).

More generally an exact graph search lists the entire rationally connected Richelot component by exhausting the Galois-stable matchings at each vertex and identifying targets up to rational curve isomorphism. Every vertex has a principal polarization and good reduction outside the same finite prime set at the level of Jacobians. Faltings's finiteness theorem for polarized abelian varieties makes the exhaustive search finite. An optional vertex budget returns an explicitly incomplete result. With geometric End $=\mathbb Z$, the degree of an isogeny delivered by a rational path is the fourth power of an integer times the degree of the primitive generator. The generator is rational because the nonzero path is rational. At a starting vertex without rational nonzero 2-torsion, this generator's characteristic kernel filtration gives a rational path of length exactly its multiplier exponent. Hence shortest-path distance $r$ gives primitive degree $4^r$. The all-odd exclusion transports across the entire component because every edge has 2-power degree and induces isomorphisms on odd Tate modules. ## Arithmetic progression and semistability

In the family of the main theorem put $g_t=x^2+tx+t^2+a$, for $t^3-t-1=0$. The source is its norm and the dual comes from its trace-dual quadratic. The source sextic, in descending order, has coefficients
$$
[1,0,3a+1,-2,3a^2+3a+1,-3a-1,a^3+2a^2+a+1].
$$
The rational Richelot dual has coefficients
$$
[1,0,-3a-5,-2,3a^2+6a+4,-2(3a+2),1-a^3-a^2].
$$
The three branch quadratics have determinant square $-23$, independently of $a$. At $a=1$, reductions at 17 and 29 certify geometric End $=\mathbb Z$ via the unique real quadratic subfields $\mathbb Q(\sqrt{37})$ and $\mathbb Q(\sqrt5)$. These witnesses persist on $a\equiv1\pmod{493}$. A nonconstant absolute invariant separates infinitely many members. Explicitly the ratio of the fifth power of the sixth self-transvectant to the sextic discriminant differs at $1$ and $87262$, so this rational function has finite fibres even on a thinner infinite progression.

Both sextics are irreducible modulo 3 at $a=1$. A 6-cycle has exactly one invariant perfect matching. Its fixed-point-free involution must commute with the cycle and thus must be its third power. This proves the two-power statement throughout $a\equiv1\pmod{1479}$. The cubic $t^3-t-1$ has Galois group $S_3$ and its three roots are the negatives of the three branch-pair sums. Reduction at 59 has cycle type $(2,1,1,1,1)$ on the source branches. It flips exactly one pair, and conjugation supplies all three flips. The full group is $(C_2)^3\rtimes S_3$. Its pairing orbits consist of the distinguished matching, the six that share one of its pairs and the eight sharing none. These witnesses persist on $a\equiv1\pmod{29087}$.

The source is semistable at every prime outside $2,23$ for each smooth integer member. Over an algebraic closure of the residue field the three cubic roots are distinct there. If two different $g_t,g_u$ share a root, subtracting their equations forces $x=-t-u$, the third cubic root. Substitution gives $a+1$, using $t^2+tx+x^2=1$ for distinct cubic roots. For $a\ne-1$ modulo the prime the three quadratics are therefore pairwise coprime and all branch multiplicities are at most two. For $a=-1$ the reduction is exactly $(x^3-x-1)^2$, again with all multiplicities two. The projective double cover in odd characteristic has two smooth points at infinity and only ordinary nodes on these special fibres. A nodal model gives semistable reduction of its Jacobian. Resolving a node of higher thickness only inserts a chain of rational components. [Semistable reduction of curves and Jacobians](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0CDM)

The replay independently computes the symbolic resultant $\operatorname{Res}_x(g_t,g_u)=(t-u)^2(a+1)$ modulo $t^2+tu+u^2-1$, verifies the double cubic at $a=-1$, and factors the full discriminant as
$$
\operatorname{disc}_x(f_a)=-23^2(a+1)^6(64a^3+96a^2+36a+27).
$$
It also checks every parameter in $\mathbb F_p$ at each odd prime $p<98$ other than 23. Direct gcds of $f_a,f_a',f_a''$ rule out a triple root in each tested fibre, including all singular ones. The symbolic identities establish the result at every other odd prime as well.

Fix the residue of $a$ at $3,5,7,11,13,17,29,31,37,59,61$ to be 1. The same powered gcds and residual irreducibility witnesses persist. At any new prime dividing the discriminant the semistable determinant theorem still applies, apart from $2,23$ already separately covered. This proves the complete rational and geometric results on the progression of modulus $30,557,478,886,935$. In a quadratic twist the action on every torsion module is tensored with a scalar sign, so all stable-submodule conditions remain the same. Twisting the maps realizes the same rational degrees.
