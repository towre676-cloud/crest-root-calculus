# Hecke packets of the residual full-spectrum collisions

The complete determinant-200,000 census has 280 opposite-secant pairs with the same full geometric elliptic quotient spectrum. Neighbour isometries account for 159 pairs. For 108 more, a single local square-class exclusion accounts for the entire signed neighbour theta sum. The remaining 13 have nonzero ordinary coefficients at indices that are squares at every prime of their theta level. All 13 can now be explained by exact Hecke decomposition. Their central components belong to eight weight-two newform orbits, every one with a certified central zero for the prescribed twist. Four of the 13 sums also have components supported away from the square class of $\mathbb Q_2$. [First two packets and local-square certificates](local-square.md)

## Exact partition of the eleven remaining sums

Use the signed sum $h=\theta_{M_1}+\theta_{M_2}-\theta_{M_3}-\theta_{M_4}$ of the four parent-neighbour theta series. The genus terms cancel. We split $h$ by $T(3^2)$, with one exception. The parent of determinant 11,440 uses $T(7^2)$, which separates its two packets. Kneser's good-prime rule acts on each lattice theta series by summing the theta series of its neighbours, with multiplicities. The rule gives a finite integral matrix on the computed neighbour closure. [Parent transfer and the Kneser convention](README.md#reduction-to-the-parent-genus) · [Good-prime operator](../../good-degree-spectrum/README.md)

The replay finds the first relation among the ordinary coefficient columns of the consecutive powers of the splitting operator. It checks each column to the weight-two order bound at the theta level, which certifies weight-$3/2$ identities after multiplication by the unary theta series with constant one. Thus the monic relation is the exact minimal polynomial of the cyclic subspace generated by $h$. Its irreducible factors are distinct in every case. The Chinese remainder projectors split $h$ into blocks indexed by these factors.

For a central block the replay certifies $T(p^2)h_P=f_p(T(g^2))h_P$, where $g$ is the splitting prime and $f_p$ is the recorded polynomial. The other good operators commute with $T(g^2)$, so the same relation holds on the whole cyclic block. Among all weight-two cuspidal newform orbits at conductors dividing half the theta level, the fingerprint selects a unique orbit. The $T(3^2)$ spectrum of each central block excludes the unary theta eigenvalues $\pm4$. Thus it belongs to the claimed cuspidal Shimura packet. The remaining blocks pass the all-valuation $\mathbb Q_2$ test of the previous study. [Replay and exact outputs](residual-packets.json)

## Exact central values on whole Galois orbits

Let $\chi$ be the quadratic ternary theta character and write $\chi_0=\chi(-1/\cdot)$. In each listed packet the twist discriminant $D$ is the fundamental discriminant of the negative of the parent determinant square class. We compute a rational basis of the odd cuspidal new modular-symbol subspace cut out by the same Hecke fingerprint. For each basis vector $s$ we evaluate the rational expression
$$
 \sum_{a\bmod |D|}\left(\frac{D}{a}\right)
 s\bigl([\infty]-[a/|D|]\bigr).
$$
The Gauss-sum formula identifies its vanishing with central $L$-value vanishing. The character of negative discriminant kills the even-symbol contribution. Applying the test to every basis vector proves the result for every normalized eigenform in the Galois orbit. The sum follows from the integral of the Fourier expansion after the finite Gauss-sum identity. If an Euler factor is omitted at a shared ramified prime, it is nonzero at the centre by the weight-two local coefficient bounds, so the vanishing is unchanged. ## Passing the central zero to every square coefficient

Here is a uniform way to meet the two-adic hypothesis of Waldspurger's classical coefficient formula in this setting. Suppose $h$ belongs to the nonunary cuspidal Shimura packet of $F$ and $L(F\otimes\chi_0^{-1},1)=0$. For each $j\ge0$ put $h_j=U(4)^j h$. The good Hecke operators commute with $U(4)$, and $h_j$ has character $\chi$ on units. Choose the even character $\psi$ of conductor 32 with $\psi(5)=\zeta_8$. Its square is trivial on $17$ and takes the value $-1$ on $25$, so it has conductor 16. Since the original quadratic $\chi_0$ has two-adic conductor at most eight, $\chi_0\psi^2$ has conductor divisible by 16.

The coefficient twist $h_j\otimes\psi$ has character $\chi\psi^2$ and good Hecke eigenvalues belonging to $F\otimes\psi^2$. The two character assertions follow from the additive-translate Gauss sum and the good Hecke formula, respectively. If the twisted half-integral form is nonzero, its existence in this packet gives Flicker's condition (H1). The conductor of $\chi_0\psi^2$ gives (H2)(b). The central factor for squarefree part one is
$$
 L\big((F\otimes\psi^2)\otimes(\chi_0\psi^2)^{-1},1\big)
 = L(F\otimes\chi_0^{-1},1)=0.
$$
Waldspurger's coefficient factorization now gives a zero at every odd square of $h_j\otimes\psi$. The conclusion also holds if that twist is zero. The values of $\psi$ on odd squares are units, so the odd square coefficients of $h_j$ vanish. Letting $j$ vary covers all squares of $h$. This proof uses no analytic-rank computation and treats all the stated central blocks uniformly. [Flicker and Waldspurger's hypotheses and formula, Purkait, Theorems 4.3.1 and 4.3.4](https://wrap.warwick.ac.uk/id/eprint/50236/1/WRAP_THESIS_Purkait_2012.pdf) · [Half-integral $U$ level formula](https://doi.org/10.4064/aa250119-3-7)

The packet decomposition, local certificates and central-symbol evaluations explain every equality in the original 280-pair census. Uniform prediction of these mechanisms beyond the determinant cutoff remains open.
