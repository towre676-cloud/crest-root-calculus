# The 39th point over B = Q(i, sqrt 7): reduction to one Gaussian projection problem

**Status.** The reduction below and the rank and saturation statements are proved by exact computation. That the projection set is exactly the one listed is a conjecture. It is supported by a search through every point \(aG_1+bG_2+T\) with \(|a|,|b|\le 20\), and the relative Chabauty step that would prove it is not implemented. The question of whether a 39th point exists stays open.

## Notation

Write \(k=\mathbb Q(i)\), \(K=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-3})\) and \(L=kK=\mathbb Q(\zeta_{12})\). Let \(\tau\) be the automorphism of \(L\) that fixes \(i\) and sends \(\sqrt{-3}\) to \(-\sqrt{-3}\), and let \(\sigma\) be the one that fixes \(\sqrt{-3}\) and sends \(i\) to \(-i\). The field \(L\) has class number 1. For \(S\) the primes above \(2,3,7\), the group \(L(S,2)\) has dimension 6, with basis \(\zeta_{12},\zeta_{12}-1\) and four \(S\)-units.

## Step 1: squareclass descent over \(L\)

Let \((s,A,D)\in k^3\) satisfy \(A^2=s^4+3\) and \(7D^2=s^4+27\). Then \(s^2+\sqrt{-3}=\delta\alpha^2\) and \(s^2+3\sqrt{-3}=\gamma\beta^2\) with \(\delta,\gamma\in L(S,2)\). Since \(A\) and \(D\) lie in \(k\), \(\delta\,\tau(\delta)\) is a square and \(\gamma\,\tau(\gamma)\) is \(7\) times a square. These two conditions leave 256 of the 4096 pairs.

The residue-field conditions at every prime of \(L\) above \(5,7,\dots,43\) are applied one prime of \(k\) at a time. They require the four quantities \(s^4+3\), \(7(s^4+27)\), \(\delta(s^2+\sqrt{-3})\) and \(\gamma(s^2+3\sqrt{-3})\) to be squares, as \(s\) runs over the projective line of each residue field of \(k\). After them, 48 pairs remain. The residue rings \(\mathcal O_L/9\), \(\mathcal O_L/8\) and \(\mathcal O_L/16\), with primitive pairs \((u,w)\), leave exactly two:
\[
(\delta,\gamma)=(\zeta_{12},\ \zeta_{12}^3+2\zeta_{12})\quad\text{and}\quad(\zeta_{12},\ 2\zeta_{12}^3+\zeta_{12}).
\]
Every elimination uses only necessary conditions, so each one is a proof. The automorphism \(\sigma\) exchanges the two survivors, matching \(s\mapsto\bar s\). The known solutions \(s=\pm1\) lie in the first class and \(s=\pm i\) in the second. [`sieve.py`]

The product \(\theta=\delta\gamma\) is \(\theta_1=3\zeta_6-1\) in the first class and \(\theta_2=3\zeta_6-2\) in the second. Both are primes of \(K\) above \(7\), with \(\bar\theta_1=-\theta_2\). Put \(\eta=\theta\sqrt{-3}\in K\). The point \((s,\theta\alpha\beta)\) on \(w^2=\theta(s^2+\sqrt{-3})(s^2+3\sqrt{-3})\) maps by \(x=\theta s^2\), \(y=\theta s w\) to
\[
E_\theta:\ y^2=x(x+\eta)(x+3\eta).
\]
This is the quadratic twist by \(\eta\) of \(y^2=x(x+1)(x+3)\), and it is defined over \(K\). This proves the reduction. **Every solution gives a point \(P\in E_{\theta_1}(L)\) with \(u(P)=x(P)/\theta_1=s^2\in k\), up to replacing \(s\) by \(\bar s\).** A 39th point therefore needs such a \(P\) with \(u(P)\notin\mathbb Q\).

## Step 2: exact ranks

A complete 2-descent over \(K\) computes Selmer groups of order 8 for \(E_{\pm\theta_1}\), \(E_{\pm\theta_2}\) and the companion curves \(E'_{\pm\theta'}\), where \(\theta'=\delta\tau(\gamma)=\zeta_6-3\) and \(E'\) has model \(y^2=x(x+\eta')(x-3\eta')\). The local images are generated by local points until they reach their known order. On 20 twists over \(\mathbb Q\) the same code agrees with PARI's `ellrank`. Each of the six curves also has an explicit point of infinite order. Hence each has rank exactly 1 over \(K\). Since \(L=K(i)\), this gives \(\operatorname{rank}E_\theta(L)=\operatorname{rank}E_\theta(K)+\operatorname{rank}E_{-\theta}(K)=2\), and likewise for \(E'\). [`descent.py`, `rank_K.py`, `points_K.py`, `check_Q.py`]

The torsion of \(E_\theta(L)\) is \(E[2]\). Let \(G_1\in E_\theta(K)\) and let \(G_2\) be the image of a generator of \(E_{-\theta}(K)\) under \((X,Y)\mapsto(-X,iY)\). The group \(\Gamma=\langle G_1,G_2\rangle+E[2]\) is 2-saturated in \(E_\theta(L)\). All 15 nonzero classes of \(\Gamma/2\Gamma\) have nontrivial Kummer image over \(L\), so the index \([E_\theta(L):\Gamma]\) is odd. [`mw_search.py`]

This rules out the shortcut \(E_\theta(L)=E_\theta(K)\). That equality would have given \(u\in K\cap k=\mathbb Q\) at once. The companion curve has the same rank, so it does not help either.

## Step 3: the remaining obligation and the evidence

It remains to prove that
\[
\{P\in E_{\theta_1}(L):u(P)\in k\}\ \text{has projection set}\ \{\infty,0,1,-9\}
\]
(and \(\{\infty,0,-1,9\}\) for \(\theta_2\)). All four values have \(s^2\in\mathbb Q\). So this statement is equivalent to the 38-point list being complete.

The set \(\{\infty,0,1,-9\}\) is exactly the projection set certified by Magma over \(\mathbb Q\). The search covered every \(aG_1+bG_2+T\) with \(|a|,|b|\le20\) and \(T\in E[2]\), which is 6724 points per class with canonical heights up to about \(3.8\times10^3\). It found no other \(u\) in \(k\). This is evidence on a subgroup of odd index and is not a proof.

The relative elliptic Chabauty step sits exactly at the dimension bound: \(\operatorname{rank}=2=[L:\mathbb Q]-[k:\mathbb Q]\). Take \(p\equiv1\pmod{12}\), for instance \(p=13\), with embeddings \(\varphi_1,\varphi_2=\varphi_1\tau,\varphi_3,\varphi_4=\varphi_3\tau\). The condition \(u\in k\) becomes \(\varphi_1(u)=\varphi_2(u)=t_1\) and \(\varphi_3(u)=\varphi_4(u)=t_2\). The logarithm vector \((\lambda_1,\dots,\lambda_4)\) of a global point lies in the 2-dimensional span \(V\) of the logarithms of \(G_1,G_2\). If \(V\) is a graph over its first two coordinates, the condition reads \((\lambda_3,\lambda_4)(t_2)=M(\lambda_1,\lambda_2)(t_1)\). In any residue disk where \(\lambda_3\) has invertible derivative, the first equation solves for \(t_2\) as an analytic function of \(t_1\). The second then gives a single function of \(t_1\), whose zeros Strassmann's theorem bounds.

Disks without known points then need a Mordell–Weil sieve. The sieve needs the odd part of \([E_\theta(L):\Gamma]\) to be controlled, through a lower bound on canonical heights over \(L\). Those two steps, the 13-adic analysis and the odd saturation, are what remain. Magma's `Chabauty` intrinsic takes only a rational projection, so it does not apply directly to this Gaussian projection.
