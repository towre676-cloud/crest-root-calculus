# Degree-four elliptic quotients over number fields

The [rational inverse](galois-fibres.md) recovers every optimal degree-four map \(C\to E\) that is defined over \(\mathbb Q\). This note removes that restriction. From a rational quintic or sextic alone, the solver returns one representative of every Galois orbit of geometric optimal degree-four maps. Each representative is written over its exact field of definition \(L\), and the orbit has \([L:\mathbb Q]\) conjugate maps. No splitting field is formed at any stage. Every output is checked by the polynomial identity \(S^6g(N/S^2)=fH^2\) in \(L[x]\), and the pulled-back differential is checked to be holomorphic. On the repeated-factor curves \(T_1,T_2\) the rational solver finds two maps. The new solver also finds a conjugate pair defined over \(\mathbb Q(i)\). The resulting count of four geometric classes agrees with the independent finite-kernel spectrum. On all 36 Richelot neighbours, on \(C,C'\), on the irreducible example and on an \(S_6\) control, the geometric counts agree with independent completeness or absence certificates. ## Why the rational argument extends

The classification in the [rational study](README.md#optimality-and-the-entire-kernel) is geometric. A degree-four map is optimal exactly when its Weierstrass distribution is \(2+2+2+0\). Since the hyperelliptic involution induces \(-1\) on the Jacobian, the four branch values form one coset of \(E[2]\). The empty one is determined by the map, so placing the target origin there fixes the map up to sign. The paired-fibre chart then normalizes the abscissa \(N/S^2\) up to the affine group, and each elliptic subvariety of degree four gives exactly one chart point. The [full-rank theorem](galois-fibres.md) excludes the rank-loss case \(c_1=c_2=0\) for every genuine cover. None of these statements uses a base field. The only rational inputs in the earlier solver were two choices. It enumerated pairings fixed by the whole absolute Galois group, and it extracted rational points of the incidence. A map defined over \(L\) has a pairing fixed by \(\mathrm{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb Q}/L)\). Its chart point is an \(L\)-point of the incidence over the field of definition \(K\subseteq L\) of the pairing. Both restrictions can therefore be replaced by exact algebra over \(K\) and its finite extensions.

## Pairings without a splitting field

A partition of the six roots of \(f\) into pairs is the same thing as a fixed-point-free involution \(\sigma\) of the étale algebra \(A=\mathbb Q[x]/(f)\). Writing \(\sigma(x)\) as a polynomial of degree at most five, the conditions are exact. The polynomial \(f(\sigma(x))\) vanishes modulo \(f\), \(\sigma(\sigma(x))\equiv x\), and \(\gcd(x-\sigma(x),f)=1\). The first says that \(\sigma\) permutes the roots, the second that the permutation is an involution, and the third that it has no fixed root. Over a field \(K\) such a \(\sigma\) is exactly a pairing stable under \(\mathrm{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb Q}/K)\). Its trace \(x+\sigma(x)\) and norm \(x\sigma(x)\) in \(A_K\) replace the rational trace and norm functions of the earlier solver.

The search for these involutions is numerical and the acceptance is exact. Let \(\ell\) be the leading coefficient of the primitive integral multiple of \(f\) and put \(\rho_i=\ell r_i\), so that the \(\rho_i\) are algebraic integers also when \(f\) has nonintegral rational coefficients. For each of the 15 pairings put
\[
\theta_P=\sum_{\{i,j\}\in P}\bigl(\rho_i\rho_j+k(\rho_i+\rho_j)\bigr)^3 ,
\]
with the least \(k\ge1\) that separates the 15 values. The resolvent \(R=\prod_P(z-\theta_P)\) has integer coefficients. It is rounded from 400-digit roots, and the precision doubles whenever rounding is not clean or \(R\) is not squarefree. The irreducible factors of \(R\) over \(\mathbb Q\) correspond to Galois orbits of pairings. For a factor of degree \(d\), the coefficients of \(\sigma\) are interpolated as polynomials in \(\theta\) over the \(d\) members of the orbit. They are recognized as rationals and rewritten in the generator of a reduced polynomial for \(K=\mathbb Q[w]/(R_j)\).

The recognized \(\sigma\) is then tested by the three identities in \(K[x]\). Acceptance needs one more exact statement, that the \(\sum d\) conjugates of all certified involutions are pairwise distinct over \(\overline{\mathbb Q}\), within an orbit and across orbits. Fix a rational functional \(\lambda\) of the six coefficients of \(\sigma\) and let \(\chi_j\in\mathbb Q[z]\) be the characteristic polynomial of \(\lambda(\sigma_j)\) over \(K_j\). Equal involutions have equal \(\lambda\)-values, so a squarefree product \(\prod_j\chi_j\) proves distinctness. The program accepts only when the orbit sizes sum to 15 and this product is squarefree for one of four fixed functionals. The 15 pairings are then all certified exactly, each with its field of definition, and no error can occur silently in the floating-point stage. The replay also checks that the test rejects the certified list of \(T_1\) with one orbit repeated. The irreducible test sextic has orbits of sizes \(1,2,3,3,6\). The sextic \(x^6-x-1\) has one orbit of size 15 over a field of degree 15. In both cases the computation takes well under a second. The earlier filter, which required the Galois group order to divide 48, is no longer used.

## Elimination over the pairing field

For one representative \(\sigma\) of each orbit, the solver builds the paired cubic algebra \(B_K=K[\gamma]\) with a generator \(\gamma=\mathrm{tr}+k\,\mathrm{nm}\) of rank three. It writes the same chart equations as the rational solver. For \(S\in\{1,x+a,x^2+ax+b\}\) these are \(N=c_2N_1-c_1N_2\), the branch value \(c_2u-c_1u^2\in B_K\), and the vanishing of the three coefficients of the residual discriminant in \(B_K\). The degeneracy locus is kept as a list of factors: \(\operatorname{Res}(S,f)\), \(\operatorname{Res}(S,N)\), the discriminant of the characteristic cubic of the branch value, and, for \(\deg S<2\), the coefficient of \(x^4\) in \(N\). A factor that vanishes identically discards the whole chart. This happens for every chart of the curve \(C\), whose optimal classes have degree two.

The intersection uses PARI arithmetic over \(K\). The common divisor of the equations is computed first. With one unknown, for \(\deg S=1\), this divisor is the solution set itself. It is saturated by the degeneracy factors and factored over \(K\), and its nondegenerate roots are kept. With two unknowns, any factor of it outside the degeneracy locus would be a positive-dimensional family of degree-four maps, and the program stops on that case. The quotients by the common divisor have finitely many common zeros. For two unknowns the projection to \(a\) is the greatest common divisor of the resultants of the first equation with each of the others. Requiring every equation to cut the projection removes the spurious pairwise intersections. For the rational pairing of \(T_1\) this reduces a resultant of degree 64 to a projection of degree 20 with five irreducible factors. Each irreducible factor over \(K\) is first examined in the relative extension \(K[z]/(\phi)\). The common divisor of the specialized equations in \(b\) is computed, and every root shared with a degeneracy factor is removed. Only a surviving fibre is passed to an absolute field, formed by `rnfequation` and reduced by `polredbest`. For \(x^6-x-1\) the projection over the degree-15 pairing field has an irreducible factor of degree 4. In the relative extension its fibre lies entirely in the degeneracy locus, so no field of degree 60 is ever formed.

Each surviving point gives a cover over its absolute field \(L\). The polynomial \(\sum g_iN^iD^{3-i}\) is divided by \(f\). The quotient must equal a scalar times a monic square, and its square root is extracted coefficientwise and checked. A shift and scaling give a short model. Over \(\mathbb Q\) this is followed by a rational rescaling minimal at every prime. Over a field of degree at most eight it is followed by division by the largest principal ideal whose fourth and sixth powers divide \(a_4,a_6\). The identity \(S^6g(N/S^2)=fH^2\) is then asserted in \(L[x]\). The pullback of \(dX/Y\) equals \(\omega\,dx/y\) with \(\omega=(N'S-2NS')/H\). The verifier requires exact divisibility and \(\deg\omega\le1\), so the cover pulls the invariant differential back to a holomorphic form. A Galois orbit of covers is determined by the irreducible factors over \(K\), so its size is \([L:\mathbb Q]\). Since the map determines its chart point and conversely, \(L\) is the field of definition of the class and not merely a field containing it.

## The repeated-factor curves

For
\[
T_1:\ y^2=154x^6-90x^5-1605x^4-900x^3+5268x^2+7200x-1792
\]
the pairings form orbits of sizes \(1,2,2,2,8\) over \(\mathbb Q\), \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt{10})\), \(\mathbb Q(i)\), \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-10})\) and \(\mathbb Q(w)\) with \(w^8+7w^4+1=0\). Only the rational pairing carries maps, and it carries four geometric maps. Two are rational and agree with the rational solver. They have half-poles \(x^2+8\) and \(x^2-10\) and targets
\[
E_1:Y^2=X^3-105903X+11165202,\qquad E_2:Y^2=X^3-105903X-11165202 .
\]
Their differentials are proportional to \(dx/y\) and \(x\,dx/y\). The two targets are quadratic twists of each other by \(-1\), with \(j=148176/25\). The other two maps form one orbit over \(\mathbb Q(i)\). The representative has
\[
S=x^2+\frac{18i-30}{17}\,x-4,\qquad E_3:Y^2=X^3+196677X-63269478,\qquad j(E_3)=\frac{237276}{625},
\]
and its conjugate is a second map to the same rational curve \(E_3\). The numerator and lift have coefficients in \(\mathbb Z[i]\) with denominators \(17^2\) and \(17^3\). They are listed in the receipt. The pulled-back differential is \((-2x+4i)\,dx/y\), proportional to \(x-2i\). The conjugate has \(x+2i\). The verifier checks that \(\omega(0)^2/\omega'(0)^2=-4\) is rational and negative. These are the diagonal directions \(i_1\pm\rho\,i_2\), where the identification \(\rho:E_1\cong E_2\) needs \(\sqrt{-1}\). This explains why the two remaining degree-four classes exist geometrically but have no rational model. The [finite-kernel certificate](../exact-completeness/README.md) for \(T_1\) gives exactly four classes of degree four in its spectrum \(d(a,b)\). The two counts agree, so the inverse solver recovers the entire degree-four part of the geometric spectrum of \(T_1\) from the equation. \(T_2\) behaves identically. Its rational maps have half-poles \(x^2+2\) and \(x^2-8/5\), and its conjugate pair has half-pole \(x^2+\frac{18i+30}{17}x-4\) with target \(Y^2=X^3+196677X+63269478\), the \(-1\)-twist of \(E_3\).

## A pairing that is not Galois-stable

For \(y^2=x^6+1\) the 15 pairings form orbits of sizes \(1,1,1,2,2,2,2,4\) over \(\mathbb Q\), \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt3)\), \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-3})\) and \(\mathbb Q(\zeta_{12})\). The solver returns 12 geometric optimal degree-four maps in six conjugate pairs, all over \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-3})\) with targets of \(j=54000\). Two pairs come from one rational pairing. The other four come from a single orbit of two conjugate pairings defined over \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-3})\) and not over \(\mathbb Q\). Each of the two pairings carries four of these eight maps. The second orbit over \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-3})\) carries none. One representative has half-pole \(x^2+(\omega-2)x+2-2\omega\) with \(\omega^2-\omega+1=0\). Such a map cannot be found by any search over Galois-stable pairings. For every returned cover the program now asserts exactly that \(N(x)S(\sigma(x))^2-N(\sigma(x))S(x)^2\equiv0\) modulo \(f\) over the point field, with \(\sigma\) carried into \(L\) by the same embedding as the chart point. The abscissa is therefore constant on each certified pair. Since the three branch values are distinct, the fibre partition of the Weierstrass points is exactly the stated pairing. This replaces, for every map, a check against the six branch points. The example first exposed a defect in the model rescaling, which took a valuation at the factor \(-1\) of a negative polynomial content. The content is now taken in absolute value.

For \(x^6-x-1\) the replay recomputes the Weil polynomial \(U^4+9U^3+41U^2+117U+169\) at the good prime 13, and its \(240\)-th power is irreducible. The Jacobian therefore has no elliptic quotient of any degree, and the solver's empty answer is forced. On every input the rational subset of the output is compared with the rational solver. The half-poles and target \(j\)-invariants agree exactly, and in the sweep the rational half-poles agree with the archived receipt. Thus no earlier rational result changes. The runs take between 0.1 and about 60 seconds on one core. The degree-two chart dominates, through resultants over the pairing field.

## Replay and scope

From this directory, with SymPy, cypari2 and python-flint installed:

    python -B number_field_fibres.py '[-1792,7200,5268,-900,-1605,-90,154]'
    python -B verify_number_fields.py
    python -B verify_number_fields.py --sweep

The first command prints the pairing orbits, the number of geometric maps and every orbit representative over its field. Fields are polynomials in \(w\), and elements are polynomials in \(w\) modulo it. The verifier replays the nine curves, compares them with the rational solver and the absence certificate, and checks the stored receipt. The `--sweep` option adds the 36 neighbours. `--write` regenerates the receipt.

The output is complete in the following sense. Every geometric optimal degree-four map lies in exactly one paired chart. Every pairing is certified with its field of definition. Every algebraic chart point outside the degeneracy locus is found, and every returned point passes the exact identity. The program stops rather than answering when the residual incidence has a positive-dimensional component outside the degeneracy locus. No test input reaches that case, but its absence is not proved in general. Maps whose pairing is not Galois-stable occur on \(y^2=x^6+1\), an example supplied by C. See the [section below](#a-pairing-that-is-not-galois-stable). The signed 16-point kernel graph of the [rational study](README.md#optimality-and-the-entire-kernel) is computed only for pairs of rational maps. For a conjugate pair over \(L\), the same Mumford comparison would run over \(L\) and the abscissa fields above it. That extension is not implemented here.
