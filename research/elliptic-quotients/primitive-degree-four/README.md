# A primitive degree-four elliptic map from a plane quartic

Given only the coefficients of a smooth plane quartic, the [search program](search.py) can discover a rational degree-four map to an elliptic curve and certify that it has no intermediate curve over the algebraic closure. On the [first input](ciani.json) it finds the construction with center height one. On the [second input](sheared.json), whose coordinates mix all three variables, it recovers the map from the coefficients at height two. The same proof gives infinitely many geometrically nonisomorphic examples.

## The map

Take the integer $a=3$. The construction also works for every integer $a$ outside $\{-1,0,1,2\}$. Set $E_a:y^2=x(x-1)(x-a)$ and let $C_a$ be the plane quartic

$$
\big(aU^2+(a-1)V^2+(2a^2-2a-1)W^2\big)\big(V^2-U^2+3W^2\big)=(2a-1)^2W^4 .
$$

On $W=1$ put $r=(aU^2+(a-1)V^2+2a^2-2a-1)/(2a-1)$. The two points and their sum are

$$
P=(r,rV),\qquad Q=(r+1,rU),\qquad f=P+Q\quad\text{on }E_a .
$$

In ordinary affine coordinates set $m=r(U-V)$. The sum has $x_f=m^2+a-2r$ and $y_f=m(r-x_f)-rV$. These rational functions determine a morphism on the smooth projective curve. Its degree is four. At $a=3$ the source polynomial is $-3U^4+U^2V^2-2U^2W^2+2V^4+17V^2W^2+8W^4$.

To obtain the plane model, form the fibre product of the double covers with function fields generated by $y_1^2=r(r-1)(r-a)$ and $y_2^2=(r+1)r(r+1-a)$. Their branch sets in the projective $r$-line are $B_1=\{0,1,a,\infty\}$ and $B_2=\{-1,0,a-1,\infty\}$. The sets meet exactly at $0,\infty$ for the given parameters. The two squareclasses are independent. The connected normalization is a degree-four Klein-four cover with six branch values, each of inertia order two. Riemann–Hurwitz gives $2g-2=-8+6\cdot2=4$. Put $U=y_2/r$ and $V=y_1/r$. Then

$$
U^2=r+(2-a)+(1-a)/r,\qquad V^2=r-(1+a)+a/r .
$$

Solving for $r$ and $1/r$ gives the quartic. The inverse formulas $y_1=rV$, $y_2=rU$ identify the two function fields. More explicitly, on $W=1$ the product of the two linear expressions in $U^2,V^2$ is a nonzero constant and both expressions are invertible. That affine part is the biquadratic model over $r\ne0$ by the displayed inverse formulas. Its coordinate ring embeds in the biquadratic function field, since over the Laurent polynomial ring it is free on $1,U,V,UV$. The homogenized equation has no line component at $W=0$. Its integral plane model has arithmetic genus three and its normalization has genus three, proving geometric smoothness.

## Degree and primitivity

Write $P,Q$ also for the maps to $E_a$. Each map has degree two. The involution changing the sign of $U$ fixes $P$ and acts by negation on $Q$. Hence $P_*Q^*=-P_*Q^*$ on $E_a$, so this homomorphism vanishes. The same holds for $Q_*P^*$. Pullback and pushforward for the sum correspond to the sums of the induced Jacobian maps. Thus $f_*f^*=[2]+[2]=[4]$, proving $\deg(f)=4$.

If $f$ factored through an isogeny of degree two, $f^*$ would kill a nonzero 2-torsion point. In fact $P_*f^*=[2]$ shows $\ker(f^*)\subseteq E_a[2]$. Work over $\overline{\mathbb Q}$. For $e\in\{0,1,a\}$ the function $x-e$ has divisor $2[(e,0)]-2[O]$ on $E_a$. The class $f^*((e,0)-O)$ vanishes exactly when $(r-e)/(r+1-e)$ is a square on $C_a$. Indeed this quotient gives twice the difference of the pulled-back divisors, and addition agrees with subtraction for these 2-torsion classes.

A rational function of $r$ becomes square in the biquadratic extension exactly when its squareclass belongs to the span of the two defining cubics. This follows from the three quadratic intermediate fields. On the base projective line the odd-valuation supports of these classes are $\varnothing,B_1,B_2,B_1\mathbin{\triangle}B_2$, of sizes zero or four. For each $e$ the tested ratio has exactly two odd places, $e$ and $e-1$. All three tests exclude vanishing. Thus $\ker(f^*)=0$ and no isogeny factor exists.

For full geometric indecomposability, an intermediate curve in a degree-four factorization would have both degrees equal to two and genus at least one. A genus-one intermediate would give the isogeny already excluded, after translation. A genus-two intermediate would require a fixed-point-free involution by Riemann–Hurwitz. Every automorphism of a smooth plane quartic acts projectively on its canonical plane. In characteristic zero, every nonidentity projective involution fixes a line, which meets the quartic. Higher genus intermediates are excluded by the same genus formula.

The parameter varies the target invariant $j(E_a)=256(1-a+a^2)^3/(a^2(1-a)^2)$. Each $E_a$ already occurs as a **degree-two** quotient via $P$. A fixed genus-three curve has finitely many automorphisms and consequently finitely many degree-two quotient isomorphism classes. The varying invariant therefore gives infinitely many geometrically nonisomorphic source curves.

For every coprime pair $m,n$, the maps $[m]P+[n]Q$ give primitive degrees $2(m^2+n^2)$ in this integer family. On the progression $a\equiv4\pmod {15}$ the three quotient directions also give a complete count of all primitive elliptic maps in arbitrary degree. ## Ramification and geometric monodromy

The geometric monodromy group of every cover in the integer family is $S_4$. Its branch points have ramification indices $3,2,2$. This also gives a second proof of indecomposability for the family.

The three holomorphic differentials $dr/y_1$, $dr/y_2$, $r\,dr/(y_1y_2)$ are independent since their Klein-four characters are distinct. The third is the pullback of the invariant differential from the genus-one quotient with square root $y_1y_2/r$. Their ratios give the canonical coordinates $[y_2:y_1:r]=[U:V:W]$. For $f=P+Q$, the elliptic differential pulls back to $dr/y_1+dr/y_2$. Its zero divisor is therefore the line $U+V=0$ on the quartic. Restricting the plane equation to this line gives
$$
 W^2\big(3(2a-1)U^2+2(a+1)(a-2)W^2\big)=0 .
$$
The double zero at $[1:-1:0]$ has ramification index three, and $x_f$ has a pole there because $r$ has a pole and $U/W-V/W$ also has a pole. The point maps to $O$. The other two points have index two. On $W=1$ they have $r=(2a-1)/3$, $V=-U$, and their images satisfy
$$
 x_f=-\frac{(a-2)(4a+1)^2}{27},\qquad
 y_f=\frac{(2a-1)^2(4a-5)(4a+1)}{81}\,U .
$$
The coefficient of $U$ and both values of $U$ are nonzero for every allowed integer $a$. Thus the two finite branch values are distinct. In the original example $a=3$ they have $x_f=-169/27$ and $y_f=(2275/81)U$, where $U^2=-8/15$.

The connected degree-four cover has transitive geometric monodromy, so its order is divisible by four. The triple branch point gives a 3-cycle, and either simple branch value gives a transposition. A subgroup of $S_4$ with these properties must be $S_4$. Its only possible proper order would be 12, and the unique index-two subgroup $A_4$ contains no transposition.

## Recovering the construction without supplied symmetries

The program locates rational involutions either with the [height-bounded finder](../row-23640/discover.py) or by extracting every rational center from the [geometric center atlas](../involution-atlas/classify.py). The second option exhausts the rational centers without a height parameter. It then tests pairs of distinct commuting involutions. Their common eigenlines give a frame $(U,V,W)$. In that frame $s=(U/W)^2$ and $t=(V/W)^2$ satisfy a conic equation. When its quadratic part splits into two distinct rational lines, completing the product gives $(l_1+c_1)(l_2+c_2)=n$. Either factor can serve as the parameter $r$. Solving the two linear equations gives $s$ and $t$ as Laurent polynomials. The two elliptic equations are $y_1^2=r^2t$ and $y_2^2=r^2s$.

The search matches the finite branch roots under $r\mapsto hr+k$ and checks a rational $y$-scaling $L$. The recovered points are $(r,rV/W)$ and $(hr+k,LrU/W)$ on the first cubic. The program requires exactly one common finite branch value, as well as the common branch at infinity. It tests all three nonzero 2-torsion classes against the branch squareclasses. Its JSON contains the coordinate frame, the actual formulas and the odd places witnessing primitivity. Recovered coordinates may differ from the simplified family formulas above.

An unresolved output describes the scope of the selected search. The bounded mode can miss centers outside the given height. Both modes require the split branch and rational identification conditions above. Quartics without a suitable rational involution pair require another construction. In particular this gives a constructive subcase of G06. Two subsequent constructions give exhaustive criteria for primitive degree-four maps on arbitrary smooth plane quartics. ## Replay

From this folder, with Python 3.11 or later and SymPy installed, run

```sh
python search.py sheared.json --height 2
python search.py sheared.json --all-rational-centers
python verify.py
```

The [verifier](verify.py) substitutes the recovered coordinates into the original quartic, independently checks the rational-function squareclasses and verifies the symbolic identities for the whole family. A separate mixed-coordinate input gives an unresolved height-one search and a map from the complete rational-center stage. The verifier also recovers maps for $a=-2,4,5$, tests an aligned 2-torsion case that must be rejected, and runs on the proven imprimitive control from Sutherland row 23640. The [receipt](receipt.json) gives the full output.
