# Mordell–Weil ranks of the genus-37 curve

The Jacobian of the CRT curve from §61 has rational Mordell–Weil rank **between 45 and 64**. Its previously published lower bound was 12, coming from the four elliptic factors. Four of its six genus-two factors now have exact ranks. Each lower bound below comes from rational divisors and exact finite-group calculations. Each upper bound comes independently from arithmetic two-descent. [Original curve, §61](https://towre676-cloud.github.io/crest-root-calculus/#sec61) · [Verified sector bounds](receipt.json)

The four cubics in the simultaneous cover $y_i^2=f_i(x)$ are $x^3+15x^2-34x+1$, $x^3+10x^2-19x+4$, $x^3+11x^2+3x-14$ and $x^3+11x^2-11x+16$, in that order. Each nonempty set $S$ of indices gives a character curve $C_S$ with $y^2=\prod_{i\in S}f_i(x)$. The sign-change group splits the rational Jacobian up to isogeny as the product of these fifteen Jacobians. Indeed the nontrivial character eigenspaces on differentials are the pullbacks from the corresponding double covers, and their dimensions sum to 37. Mordell–Weil ranks add under this isogeny. ## Why the divisors are independent

An effective divisor $D$ is specified by monic, squarefree $u$ and $v$ with $u\mid(f-v^2)$. Its points satisfy $u(x)=0$ and $y=v(x)$. We use the global degree-zero class $D-\iota D$, where $\iota$ sends $y$ to $-y$. On the monic even-degree models the two rational points above infinity give one more possible difference. The verifier checks the polynomial identities over the rationals. [Divisors and reduction multipliers](certificate.json)

At an odd prime of good reduction, an odd-degree model gives reduced Mumford coordinates directly. For an even-degree model choose a simple rational branch root $r$ outside the supports, put $L=f'(r)$, and set $X=L/(x-r)$, $Y=yX^{g+1}/L$. The new equation is monic of degree $2g+1$. Relative to the new point at infinity the local class $D-\iota D$ is twice the class of $D$. The original two infinities map to $(0,\pm L^g)$. The program verifies each divisor on the new equation before performing Cantor addition. [Coordinate transforms](rank.py) · [Group law](mumford.py)

For each support choose two distinct good primes with positive multipliers killing all displayed reductions. Let $B$ be their product. The subgroup generated by the classes $BD_i$ is torsion-free. Any of its torsion elements reduces to zero at both primes, and at a good prime $p$ the reduction kernel has no prime-to-$p$ torsion. For each subsequent witness prime a recorded multiplier takes every $D_i$ into one cyclic group of prime order $\ell$, where $\ell\nmid B$. The verifier checks the order and reads off the coefficients by direct additions. The resulting matrix has full column rank modulo $\ell$. A primitive integral relation between the $BD_i$ would therefore have every coefficient divisible by $\ell$. This proves independence over the integers. Discovery used finite Jacobian orders to propose multipliers; the proof checks the multiplication identities themselves.

## Why the descent bounds are upper bounds

Use the étale algebra $L=\prod_{i\in S}\mathbb Q[x]/f_i$. On an odd-degree model the usual $x-\theta$ map embeds $J(\mathbb Q)/2J(\mathbb Q)$ in the square classes of $L$ of square norm. On an even-degree model we also quotient by diagonal rational scalars. Each component has degree three, so an odd-degree factor exists globally and over every completion. The monic even model has rational points at infinity. Under these conditions the descent loses no elements of $J/2J$. The cited results also describe the behavior outside the bad primes. [Poonen and Schaefer, §§11–12](https://math.mit.edu/~poonen/papers/descent.pdf) · [Stoll, §§4–6](https://www.impan.pl/en/publishing-house/journals-and-series/acta-arithmetica/all/98/3/83397/implementing-2-descent-for-jacobians-of-hyperelliptic-curves)

The finite set $T$ includes 2 and every odd prime whose square divides the polynomial discriminant for odd-degree models and the genus-two models. For genus five it includes every prime dividing the discriminant. The descent classes have representatives with even valuation everywhere outside $T$. In the even-degree case one can remove common valuation parity by a rational scalar. The code enlarges the chosen set when needed and verifies that the $T$-class numbers of all component fields are odd. Each remaining global square class therefore has a $T$-unit representative. PARI's `bnfcertify` evaluates the class and unit data unconditionally. The global matrix imposes square norm by checking sign and valuations at $T$.

At a finite place the unit classes come from discrete logarithms modulo a prime ideal $\mathfrak p$ above an odd $p$, or modulo $\mathfrak p^{2e+1}$ at 2, together with valuation parity. Hensel's lemma makes these coordinates exact. At a real place they are the signs of the real embeddings. Each generator of the local image comes from a point over the completion, a verified square in an explicit finite extension followed by norm, or the usual odd-model branch divisor formula. For a rational point the column has entries $x-\theta$. For an extension point with coordinate $t$ and characteristic polynomial $h$ over the completion it has entries $(-1)^{\deg h}h(\theta)$. Subtracting the same degree at infinity normalizes the column. The leading coefficient is one.

Let $m_p$ be the number of local field factors. The local dimension of $J/2J$ is $m_p-1$ on odd models and $m_p-2$ on even models, with an additional $g$ at 2. These formulas follow from the description of rational two-torsion by even subsets of branch points and the local formal group. Any Galois-invariant partition whose two parts are interchanged would split every global or local orbit in half. The odd-degree orbit rules out this extra invariant. At the real place with $r$ real finite roots the corresponding dimension is $(r-1)/2$ or $(r-2)/2$. The program verifies full local dimension after adjoining the scalar directions for even models. [Local divisor witnesses](local-witnesses.json) · [Exact arithmetic implementation](selmer.gp)

Each local left kernel supplies linear constraints on global unit exponents. From the dimension of their common kernel subtract the diagonal rational $T$-unit dimension $1+|T|$ on even models and the global rational two-torsion dimension on all models. Scalar square classes inject because a component has odd degree. The result is the stated rank upper bound. Matching bounds determine the exact rank in eight sectors. ## Rational torsion

The elliptic and genus-two sector Jacobians have trivial rational torsion. Each of the four genus-four sectors and the genus-five sector has rational torsion $(\mathbb Z/2)^2$. The branch subsets supply the lower bounds. For an upper bound on the $\ell$-primary torsion, take the gcd of recorded finite Jacobian orders at good primes different from $\ell$. Reduction injects on prime-to-residue-characteristic torsion. Two primes per sector suffice, and PARI recomputes every order from the curve. At 3 and 5 the Python replay independently counts the points over the first five field extensions, then recovers all 30 finite Jacobian orders by Newton identities. [Direct point counts](direct_counts.py) [Finite orders](torsion-orders.json) · [Torsion verification](torsion.py)

For the original genus-37 Jacobian the rational torsion has exponent dividing **8** and order dividing **2,048**. Write $\pi_S:X\to C_S$ for each degree-eight character projection. On $J_X$ the sum of $\pi_S^*\pi_{S*}$ is multiplication by eight. Indeed, the sum over the kernel of a character acts by eight on that character and by zero on every other nontrivial character. The trivial character contributes the Jacobian of the projective line, which is zero.

Every rational torsion element of a character sector dies under $\pi_S^*$. For an odd triple support its two-torsion is generated by the three branch blocks belonging to the individual cubics, with the branch infinity subtracted three times. Upstairs the pullback of each such difference is the divisor of $y_i$. For the four-cubic support its rational two-torsion is generated by unions of two branch blocks with three hyperelliptic fibers subtracted. Each pulls back to the divisor of $y_i y_j$. This proves $[8]T=0$ for every rational torsion point $T$ on $J_X$. Finally the product of the fifteen finite sector orders at the common good prime 41 has 2-adic valuation eleven. The character isogeny gives the same finite order for $J_X$, which yields the order bound. Its exact rational torsion group remains open.

Run `python -B verify.py` with SymPy installed. This checks every global divisor relation, finite group identity and binary descent matrix. With PARI/GP available on the path it also regenerates all four certified number fields, their units, every local Kummer image and the descent matrices. An explicit executable may be supplied with `python -B verify.py --gp /path/to/gp`. The release was checked both ways, with PARI/GP 2.17.2 for the arithmetic regeneration.
