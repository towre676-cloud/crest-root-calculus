# Cyclic quotients with several points over infinity

The input is one rational equation. The map's horizontal coordinate may now be a fraction \(A/Q\). The denominator is discovered from the equation. The search covers rational equivariant maps to the squarefree target models in the [cyclic theorem](proof.md) for which source infinity maps to target infinity, at least one finite horizontal pole is present, and all such poles lie over the finite branch locus of the working cyclic cover. Their orders may be different and may exceed the cyclic exponent. The polynomial solver covers the singleton fibre.

## Pole data from valuations

Use the chart \(y^k=B(x)\) obtained using [power removal and a cyclic character](proof.md). The polynomial \(B\) has multiplicities from one to \(k-1\) at its finite roots. Write \(m=\deg G\) for the squarefree unknown target \(z^k=G(u)\), with \(m\ge2\) and \(\gcd(m,k)=1\). Write \(d=\deg A>\deg Q=q\). Normalize \(Q,A\) to be monic, the coefficient of \(x^q\) in \(A\) to zero, and the vertical multiplier's numerator to be monic. The independent affine change of \(u\) and scaling of \(z\) have exactly this freedom, by the invariant and weight-one pole spaces at target infinity. Each normalized solution gives one map up to pointed equivariant target isomorphism. Its degree in the working chart is \(d\), by comparing the field degrees over the field generated by \(A/Q\).

At a finite pole of \(A/Q\) of order \(b\), let \(r=\operatorname{ord}(B)\). The equation \(G(A/Q)=B(z/y)^k\) implies \(mb+r\equiv0\pmod{k}\). Over a branch point \(r>0\), so \(b\) lies in a nonzero class modulo \(k\). A rational denominator uses whole rational irreducible factors of \(B\). For each selected factor \(p\) of branch multiplicity \(r_p\) and horizontal pole order \(b_p\), form
\[
Q=\prod_p p^{b_p},\quad R=\prod_p p^{r_p},\quad
D=\prod_p p^{(mb_p+r_p)/k},\quad T=\prod_p p^{b_p-1}.
\]
The vertical denominator is forced by valuation. Away from \(Q\), the factor multiplying \(y\) can have no pole since the branch multiplicity is less than \(k\) and \(G(A/Q)\) is regular. Thus \(z=yV/D\) for a polynomial \(V\) coprime to \(Q\). With \(F=B/R\) and \(G_h(A,Q)=Q^mG(A/Q)\), the identity becomes
\[
G_h(A,Q)=FV^k,\qquad D^k=Q^mR.
\]
Conversely a solution with \(A,Q\) coprime and \(G\) squarefree gives the original map and extends to the smooth projective curves. In the original coordinates multiply the vertical denominator \(D\) by the denominator already computed in the working chart.

## Degree bounds and the complementary search

At a root of \(V\), the value of \(A/Q\) is a root of \(G\), and \(G'\) is a unit there. Differentiating \(G(A/Q)=FV^k/Q^m\) gives \(V^{k-1}\mid A'Q-AQ'\). At a finite pole the Wronskian vanishes to order exactly \(b_p-1\). Hence, for monic \(W\),
\[
A'Q-AQ'=(d-q)T V^{k-1}W .
\]
Write \(e=\deg V\), \(s=\deg Q-\deg T\), \(w=\deg W\), and \(N=\deg F\). It follows that
\[
md=N+ke,\qquad w=d+s-1-(k-1)e,\qquad
[(k-1)m-k]d\le (k-1)N+k(s-1).
\]
The right-hand side is independent of the horizontal pole orders. Since \(\deg R\ge s\), it is at most \((k-1)\deg B+\deg\operatorname{rad}(B)-k\). This uniformly bounds \(d\) and, by \(q<d\), the pole orders. The target genus and Riemann–Hurwitz bound \(m\) and can reduce \(d\) further. A supplementary filter uses \(N\ge m(d\bmod k)\). Each polynomial \(A-\beta Q\), for a root \(\beta\) of \(G\), has degree \(d\). These polynomials are pairwise coprime. The total multiplicity of \(F\) supported on each of them is congruent to \(d\) modulo \(k\).

Set \(\ell=d-q\). Let \(a,c,\tau,v,b,f\) be the reversals of \(A,Q,T,V,W,F\) in \(t=1/x\), dividing \(f\) by its leading coefficient before reversal. All have constant coefficient one. The identities imply
\[
a=(fv^k)^{1/m}\pmod {t^\ell},\qquad
\ell ac-ta'c+tac'=\ell\tau v^{k-1}b.
\]
For \(1\le j\le\min(e,\ell-1)\), temporarily put the \(j\)-th coefficient of \(v\) equal to zero and compute the coefficient of order \(j\) of the difference of the Wronskian sides using the formal root. Calling that difference \(C_j\), recover
\[
v_j=\frac{m C_j}{m\ell(k-1)-k(\ell-j)}.
\]
The denominator is strictly positive. The two contributions from \(v_j\) to the difference are \((\ell-j)k v_j/m\) and \(-\ell(k-1)v_j\). The poles prescribe \(Q\) and \(T\). Coefficients of \(v\) at order at least \(\ell\) remain free in this calculation.

Recover \(A\) from the linear Wronskian equation in descending degree. The highest contribution of \(a_i x^i\) is \((i-q)a_i x^{i+q-1}\). The coefficient \(i=q\) is normalized to zero and leftover coefficients are checked. Recover \(G\) by subtracting multiples of \(A^jQ^{m-j}\) for descending \(j\), whose leading degrees \(jd+(m-j)q\) are distinct. Check the residual, the numerator–denominator resultant, and the target discriminant. The complementary search has \(w+\max(0,e-\ell+1)\) free coefficients. The direct search uses \(e+q\), since the formal root recovers the \(\ell-1\) upper coefficients of \(A\) and leaves its \(q\) lower ones free. The implementation chooses the smaller list before any inverse variable used to impose the open conditions.

The [bounded-pointed-map finiteness argument](../coefficient-quotients/proof.md#finiteness-with-an-unknown-elliptic-target) and effective de Franchis–Severi in higher target genus make the exact incidence finite after the open conditions. There are finitely many cyclic actions on each pointed positive-genus target since its pointed automorphism group is finite. The solver enumerates rational maps. A geometric support count concerns its specified rational denominator and degree triple.

## Exact examples and replay

For \(k=3\), [branch-poles.json](branch-poles.json) gives the expanded source polynomial of degree five. With denominator \(x\), the degree-five solution is
\[
A=x^5-\tfrac{68}{9}x^4+22x^3-\tfrac{92}{3}x^2-\tfrac{16}{3},
\quad V=(x-1)(x-2),\quad
G(u)=u^2+41u+\tfrac{34040}{81},\quad D=x.
\]
Source infinity and the point above \(x=0\) map to target infinity with multiplicities four and one. The complementary chart has one free coefficient versus three in the direct chart. Exact elimination of the original identity without derivatives independently gives \(V=x^2-3x+2\). Increasing the source's coefficient of \(x\) by one makes both degree strata for this denominator empty through degree five. The raw degree-five ideal becomes the unit ideal.

The replay also examines every denominator in a degree-two search on \(y^2=x(x^2+1)(x^4+x^2+1)\). It finds two maps with horizontal coordinate \(x+1/x\), to targets of genus one and genus two. Two further cubic fixtures recover one map each at the selected denominator. One has finite poles of orders one and two at different branch points. The other has a pole of order four for exponent three. All exported maps are substituted in the original coordinates. The verifier rejects a removed degree stratum.

~~~sh
python -B research/cyclic-cover-reconstruction/rational.py research/cyclic-cover-reconstruction/branch-poles.json --max-degree 5 --denominator 0 1
python -B research/cyclic-cover-reconstruction/verify_rational.py
~~~

Omit the denominator filter to search all admissible rational denominators. A certificate records the requested scope. The verifier rebuilds it from the source and filters. Exact elimination can be expensive.

For exponent two on both sides, equivariance holds for any map carrying the two points at infinity to each other. Embed each curve in its Jacobian using that point. The hyperelliptic involution acts by negation on the embedded curve, and pushforward commutes with negation. The target embedding is injective, which gives the commutative square. This is the classical pointed argument, also used by Tian et al., Lemma 1. The larger-fibre solver still requires that the finite horizontal poles be branch-supported.

[Gallese, Lombardo, Naccarato and Zannier (2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.02429) also give a direct algorithm for a complementary elliptic quotient in genus two. Their input includes a first known map, as specified in the abstract.

This follow-up used full-text searches in Frey–Kani and Hurwitz-space papers and searches for Kummer ramification, algebraic and rational decomposition, and inversion of Wronskians. The exact complementary recurrence coupling the reversed source identity to the divided Wronskian was not located in these publications. Literature priority remains open. For larger exponents the extension assumes equivariance. It covers finite poles supported on the branch locus. At unbranched finite \(x\)-values a horizontal pole order must be a multiple of \(k\), and those poles remain outside this search.
