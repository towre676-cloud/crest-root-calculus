# Geometric simple factors of dimension 80 in the tenth cubic-tower stage

Each five-element subset of the ten indices gives two abelian factors of dimension 80. Ordinary reduction at a collection of good primes bounds the dimension of any proper geometric abelian subvariety. At a separate comparison prime, the residues of a few Frobenius power sums distinguish all 504 factors up to isogeny over the algebraic closure. **All 504 five-index factors are absolutely simple and pairwise geometrically nonisogenous.** The dimension intersections are empty in every case. The proof uses 2,029 local polynomials, with its last witness at 383. The same comparison separates all 420 six-index factors, which have dimension 192. The [five-index certificate](certificate.json) and [six-index certificate](six-index.json) contain the reduced polynomials used in these conclusions.

## Powers and fixed points in the finite-field polynomial algebra

For a signed factor of dimension $d$, let $P(T)$ be its reciprocal Weil numerator, and take a simultaneous good prime $p>d$. The [finite-fibre method](../cubic-tower-geometric-separation/README.md) computes $P$ modulo $p$. Its unit-root polynomial in the variable $x$ is the monic characteristic polynomial in descending order obtained by truncating the numerator after its last nonzero coefficient. Its roots are the residues of the characteristic-zero unit roots of Frobenius, with multiplicity. An ordinary factor has exactly $d$ such roots.

Put $M_d=\mathrm{lcm}\{n:\varphi(n)\le4d^2\}$. A geometric isogeny between two equal-dimensional factors would identify their Frobenius eigenvalues after a common power. A ratio of two eigenvalues has degree at most $4d^2$, so if any power of the ratio equals one, its $M_d$th power equals one. Consequently the multiset of $M_d$th powers must agree for geometrically isogenous factors. In particular the unit-root count and the first three power sums of those powered unit roots agree modulo $p$. A difference in any entry of this four-integer signature excludes a geometric isogeny. At $p=83$ the signatures distinguish all $\binom{504}{2}=126756$ five-index pairs. At $p=193$ they distinguish all $\binom{420}{2}=87990$ six-index pairs. These separation tests apply to nonordinary factors as well.

To restrict dimensions, take an ordinary reduction. A geometric abelian subvariety of dimension $e$ would supply $e$ unit roots, with multiplicities stable under the Galois action after a finite extension. Passing back to $M_d$th powers preserves that stability because different characteristic-zero $M_d$th powers remain different after any further power. Their reductions must therefore form a union of Frobenius orbits, including permitted multiplicities. The possible $e$ are among the subset sums of the orbit sizes. Intersect these sets across the good reductions of the same factor, retaining $1\le e\le d/2$ by the existence of an isogeny complement. An empty intersection proves absolute simplicity. [Geometric specialization argument](../cubic-tower-cartier/README.md)

The [orbit calculation](unit_orbits.py) works with squarefree components of the unit-root polynomial. Successively taking gcds with $x^{p^n}-x$ on the remaining polynomial groups together all irreducibles of the same degree $n$. On such a group $h$, work in $R=\mathbb F_p[x]/h$ and compute $y=x^{M_d}$, reducing the exponent modulo $p^n-1$. For each divisor $s$ of $n$, the degree of $\gcd(h,y^{p^s}-y)$ counts the original roots whose powered image has Frobenius orbit size dividing $s$. Subtract the counts for proper divisors to obtain the count with orbit of size exactly $s$. Dividing by $s$ and restoring the squarefree multiplicity gives the number of orbits of that size counted with multiplicity. This remains valid when different original roots have the same powered image.

The algebra traces of $y,y^2,y^3$ give the comparison signature. The Newton sums of $1,x,\dots,x^{\deg(h)-1}$ supply the trace functional. Polynomial products use convolution followed by a precalculated matrix for reduction of the high powers of $x$. The Frobenius matrix on $R$ makes the fixed-point counts finite-field matrix operations. The numerical implementation reduces after each operation; its integer dot products fit in signed 64 bits for the checked dimensions and primes. The independent verifier also explicitly factors sample polynomials, computes their field traces by another method, and compares actual point counts on the tower.

In addition to separating every six-index pair, the ordinary reduction at 193 alone proves absolute simplicity for $\{1,2,3,5,8,9\}^{+}$ and $\{1,3,5,6,7,8\}^{+}$. For the two factors on $\{0,1,2,3,4,5\}$, collections of primes give separate proofs in the [sixth-stage calculation](../cubic-tower-geometric-separation/README.md). ## Batched coefficient calculation

The [second implementation](block_frobenius.py) assembles the finite-fibre matrices by splitting the support into two groups. For a required coefficient $[z^q]L(z)R(z)f(z)^e$, precompute $R(z)f(z)^e$ within each parity group. The coefficient is a dot product between a contiguous slice of the coefficients of $L$ and the reversed matching slice of the second polynomial. Batched matrix multiplication gives many such coefficients at once. Its inputs are integral residues. Before each multiplication the program checks that the length of the dot product times $(p-1)^2$ is below $2^{53}$. Every product and partial sum is then an exactly representable binary64 integer. The resulting signed matrices and infinity correction compute the same local characteristic polynomials as the scalar fibre program.

The six-index build uses the scalar implementation. Its full recount uses this second assembly for all 210 subsets. As another test, the two implementations gave the same 898 coefficients on the seven-index support $\{0,1,2,3,4,5,6\}$ at 449. The complete size-five package also compares independent holomorphic differential matrices in dimension 80.

## Exclusive geometric constituents in 318 six-index factors

At 193, **318** of the 420 signed six-index factors each have a geometric simple constituent that occurs in no other six-index factor. Its dimension is bounded below by an explicit integer between 97 and 192. Twelve of the sixteen six-index factors already proved geometrically simple occur among these 318. Including the remaining four yields at least **322 distinct geometric simple classes** from six-index supports. The 1,275 classified factors on supports of sizes at most five, including the base, have dimension at most 80. The two geometrically simple seven-index factors have dimension 448. Thus the stage-ten Jacobian has **at least 1,599 distinct geometric simple isogeny classes**, with combined dimension at least **102,691**. The number of individually certified whole simple factors is 1,293. [Six-index coefficients at 193](six-index.json) · [Seventh-stage certificates](../cubic-tower-stage-seven/README.md)

Take $d=192$ and the universal exponent $M_d$ from the [geometric Frobenius test](../cubic-tower-geometric-separation/README.md#certifying-geometric-isogeny-information). The mod-193 unit polynomial of a $d$-dimensional abelian variety has degree at most $d$. If its powered unit roots contain an orbit of degree $n>96$, its preimage must have degree $n$ as well. Indeed the powered degree divides the original degree, and the next possible multiple exceeds 192. The original unit polynomial therefore contains a unique irreducible of degree $n$, with multiplicity one. For a root $x$ of this irreducible, the checker computes $y=x^{M_d}$ in $\mathbb F_{193^n}$. It tests $y^{193^{n/\ell}}\ne y$ for each prime $\ell\mid n$ to certify that the powered orbit retains degree $n$. The degree and the field traces of $y,y^2,y^3$ distinguish the 318 reported orbits from all competing six-index factors.

To see the implication over $\overline{\mathbb Q}$, decompose a signed factor into its geometric simple constituents. Choose a finite extension at 193 over which these constituents and the isogeny specialize. For an appropriate $r$, each constituent then cuts out a rational submultiset of the characteristic-zero Weil roots raised to $rM_d$. The map from the distinct $M_d$th powers to their $r$th powers is injective and Galois equivariant. Any root-of-unity ratio between two original roots has degree at most $(2d)^2$ and order dividing $M_d$. Thus each rational submultiset lifts back to $M_d$, with multiplicities. Its monic polynomial is integral and reduction preserves products. The large residue orbit must belong to one constituent. An abelian variety of dimension $e$ has at most $e$ unit roots, by the reciprocal pairing of Weil roots, so this constituent has dimension at least $n$. Because $2n>d$, it is the unique constituent of dimension greater than 96 in its signed factor. If it also occurred in a different six-index factor, use a common finite extension for the two reductions. The same ratio bound compares roots across the two factors, so that identical constituent would give the same lifted unit orbit in both. Exclusivity of the fingerprint rules this out.

Adding the recorded orbit degrees gives 43,470 across the 318 supports. For the twelve already known to be simple, use their actual dimension 192. Include the four further simple 192-folds, the two simple 448-folds and the known lower supports, counting each once. This gives the dimension bound above. The verifier checks a digest of the exact support-to-dimension assignment as well as the totals.

Run `python high_orbits.py --workers 4` from this directory. Add `--list` to print the dimension lower bound on each of the 318 supports. The calculation uses the existing 193 polynomials and the preceding geometric simplicity certificates.
