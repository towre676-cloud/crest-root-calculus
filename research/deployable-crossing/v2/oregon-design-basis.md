# Oregon design basis and Tillamook evidence gaps

This document fixes the source basis for the Tillamook study as reviewed on **24 November 2026**. It distinguishes agency design provisions, adopted traffic-control standards, technical guidelines, mapped evidence and project proposals. It does not determine the regulatory classification of a novel mobile bridge or give a site approval.

The expanded study envelope supplied for hazard retrieval is **EPSG:26910, [433750, 5033050, 434350, 5034000] metres**. It includes the initially requested downtown box and the tested approach-offset sweep area. It is an investigation area, not a surveyed bridge, support, property or exclusion-zone boundary. The Atlas supplies spatial context; the observations below determine what additional evidence the engineering model needs. Source metadata are recorded in [oregon-sources.json](oregon-sources.json), and the actual hazard query and clipping record is in [data/hazards/manifest.json](data/hazards/manifest.json).

## Design provisions and their scope

| Subject | Verified provision | Application to this study and unresolved decision |
|---|---|---|
| Clearance under a new pedestrian overpass | ODOT HDM Part 300, January 2025, **317.3, p. 300-146**, specifies **17 ft 4 in**, excluding a buffer for future overlays. Clearance covers the roadway and usable shoulders. [HDM 300](https://www.oregon.gov/odot/Engineering/Documents_RoadwayEng/HDM-0300.pdf) | Use this as the explicit ODOT pedestrian-overpass starting criterion. Road ownership, route classification and the proposed structure's treatment must be established. Check the lowest component in its deformed position against the surveyed road envelope. A legal vehicle height of 14 ft is not a pedestrian-bridge clearance criterion. |
| Generic clearance table and construction state | ODOT BDM Section 2, October 2026, **2.14.4.2.4**, refers to HDM 317 and future pavement allowances. Its generic high-route table lists **17.25 ft**, whereas HDM's pedestrian provision lists **17 ft 4 in**. **2.14.4.2.5** separately addresses construction clearances. [BDM 2](https://www.oregon.gov/odot/Bridge/Guidance/BDM-2026-10-Section2.pdf) | Preserve the numerical discrepancy for the responsible road/bridge engineer; do not equate the values or silently add/subtract buffers. Do not use a construction-state criterion to approve occupied service. Deployment requires its own traffic-control and clearance assessment. |
| Earthquake response | ODOT BDM **1.17**, October 2026, **p. 30, Pedestrian Bridges**, directs new pedestrian bridges over vehicular roads to the Ordinary-bridge provisions of **1.17.2.3**. Its **p. 2, Non-Conventional Bridges**, calls for project-specific seismic design criteria. [BDM 1.17](https://www.oregon.gov/odot/Bridge/Guidance/BDM-2026-10-Section1-17.pdf) | A project-specific classification is required. The separate diversion-bridge provisions do not automatically apply because the proposed device has wheels or a short operating period. Ground class, foundation behavior, restraint and connection demands remain unknown. |
| Accessible work-zone route | ODOT's **February 2026 TPAR Overview, pp. 1-3**, applies to listed work-zone projects, including those on or along the state highway system. It requires continuity at least equivalent to existing accessibility. Published dimensions include **7.5% maximum ramp running slope, 1.5% cross slope**, and **60 in continuous width**, or **48 in with 60 by 60 in landings every 200 ft**. [TPAR overview](https://www.oregon.gov/odot/Engineering/Docs_TrafficEng/TPAR-Overview.pdf) | Carry the existing accessible route through setup, deployment and removal. These work-zone provisions do not authorize a new public overpass with stairs as its only access. Treat access-route geometry and public operating service as explicit design components. |
| Elevated-route accessibility | PROWAG **R201.2** addresses temporary and permanent facilities; **R203.7** addresses pedestrian overpasses. For approaches exceeding 5%, it identifies compliant ramps, elevators or limited-use elevators; the platform-lift exception concerns technically infeasible alterations. [Access Board scoping](https://www.access-board.gov/prowag/scoping.html) | PROWAG is a technical reference with adoption-dependent application. ODOT's current accessibility page states that USDOT adoption covers transit stops and that DOJ review continues. The governing adopted requirements for this facility must be recorded. An arbitrary portable platform lift is not demonstrated compliant by this citation. [ODOT adoption/applicability](https://www.oregon.gov/odot/ada/pages/engineering.aspx) |
| Deployment traffic control | ODOT states that, effective **1 June 2026**, Oregon adopted the MUTCD 11th Edition including Revision 1, the June 2026 Oregon supplement, and the January 2026 Oregon Temporary Traffic Control Handbook. The handbook addresses work zones of three days or less and its **1.5** addresses pedestrian accommodation. [Adoption](https://www.oregon.gov/odot/Engineering/Pages/MUTCD.aspx) · [2026 handbook](https://www.oregon.gov/odot/Engineering/Docs_TrafficEng/OTTCH-2026.pdf) | The operational concept needs a site-specific traffic-control plan covering all road users. A short deployment time is not evidence that setup or movement can occur over live traffic. The study does not prescribe an unapproved traffic closure. |

The deformation, settlement and positioning allowances mentioned above are engineering model requirements proposed here, not numerical allowances claimed from the cited provisions. No crowd load, wind threshold, foundation capacity, material grade, connector rating or allowable deployment time is selected by this source review.

## Tillamook locations supported by planning records

The **US 101 Main/Pacific couplet between 3rd and 12th Streets** is a documented crossing-needs corridor. ODOT's March 2025 **Tillamook TSP Technical Memorandum 2, p. 25**, summarizes the 2018 crossing study: marked continental crossings and advance signs at **7th and 9th Streets**, and a marked crossing, advance signs and a northbound RRFB at **11th Street**. These are street-level improvement recommendations, not agency recommendations for an overhead bridge. Current installation status and whether the investigation envelope includes each location must be verified from current records and geometry. [Technical memorandum](https://www.oregon.gov/odot/Projects/Project%20Documents/TillamookTSP-TechnicalMemo2.pdf)

ODOT's **Northwest Oregon Pedestrian Safety Upgrades, project 22709**, lists US 101 locations at mileposts 64.4 and 124.4 and construction in 2027. The page discusses median islands, RRFBs, lighting and curb ramps according to location. This review does not assign a milepost to an unverified cross street. Include the project in the comparison of planned interventions before claiming an unmet site need. [Current project](https://www.oregon.gov/odot/projects/pages/project-details.aspx?project=22709)

The **county** TSP update covers rural/unincorporated areas and currently schedules adoption for spring 2027. Its draft status must be retained. It does not replace the **City of Tillamook's 2019 TSP**, separately identified in the technical memorandum. The older City PDF URL was unavailable during this review, so this document does not claim to have examined that entire plan. [County update and schedule](https://www.oregon.gov/odot/projects/pages/project-details.aspx?project=TillamookTSP)

## Hazard evidence and treatment of unknowns

The capture uses envelope-intersection queries against official services, followed by complete ID controls and full XY geometry retrieval in bounded batches. Original response bytes are retained outside the repository; the shipped [raw manifest](data/hazards/raw-manifest.json) and request records preserve endpoints, parameters, timestamps and SHA-256 hashes. Queries request EPSG:26910 output without simplification or coordinate rounding. Full features can extend far outside the envelope. GDAL's ESRIJSON reader and GEOS clipping produce the compact GeoJSON supplied here, with holes and multipart geometry handled by the geometry engine. This is floating-point GIS processing, not exact geodetic computation. Clipping is restricted to the declared envelope; only further intersection with actual proposed support and route footprints answers a placement question.

| Evidence | Official source and interpretation |
|---|---|
| Effective flood mapping | [FEMA NFHL layer 28](https://hazards.fema.gov/arcgis/rest/services/public/NFHL/MapServer/28) provides flood zones and subtypes, including floodway distinctions, with available base elevations and datum fields. Preserve `FLD_ZONE`, `ZONE_SUBTY`, `SFHA_TF`, `STATIC_BFE`, `V_DATUM` and source references. Do not confuse layer 28 with water-area layer 32. Effective flood mapping is regulatory evidence, but a spatial hit alone does not decide a permit or establish a blanket construction prohibition. [NOAA description of FEMA NFHL](https://coast.noaa.gov/digitalcoast/data/flood.html) |
| Tsunami scenarios | [DOGAMI evacuation zones, layer 12](https://gis.dogami.oregon.gov/arcgis/rest/services/Public/OTGC1/MapServer/12) and [local Cascadia XXL1 inundation, layer 78](https://gis.dogami.oregon.gov/arcgis/rest/services/Public/OTGC1/MapServer/78) screen supports, access and evacuation routes. Retain the scenario identity. A crossing is not a tsunami refuge merely because it is elevated. |
| Landslide inventory and coverage | [DOGAMI deposit polygons, layer 3](https://gis.dogami.oregon.gov/arcgis/rest/services/Public/SLIDO42/MapServer/3), [detailed study coverage, layer 0](https://gis.dogami.oregon.gov/arcgis/rest/services/Public/SLIDO42_DetailedLSSus/MapServer/0), and [deep susceptibility, layer 1](https://gis.dogami.oregon.gov/arcgis/rest/services/Public/SLIDO42_DetailedLSSus/MapServer/1) answer different questions. A study-coverage hit is not a hazard polygon; missing coverage is not a low-hazard classification. DOGAMI's current data page links service URLs containing `SLIDO42` while discussing later releases, so the URL is not a reliable version identifier. [SLIDO data](https://www.oregon.gov/dogami/slido/Pages/data.aspx) |
| Historical local risk assessment | [DOGAMI IMS-58](https://pubs.oregon.gov/dogami/ims/p-ims-058.htm) includes Tillamook communities and flood-depth rasters. The assessment was performed in 2016. It is context and scenario evidence, not a substitute for current effective mapping or a current site investigation. |

DOGAMI expressly limits its interactive maps to general information and says they do not replace site investigations. **Zero returned features, query failure and absent mapping coverage are different results. None certifies hazard-free ground.** A coarse map cannot establish pad bearing, buried utilities, liquefaction resistance or acceptable settlement. [DOGAMI GIS scope and limitations](https://www.oregon.gov/dogami/gis/pages/index.aspx)

For a first controlled prototype, the project may choose to defer footprints with unresolved flooding, tsunami, landslide, subsurface or access exposure. That is a proposed project selection policy, not a new legal exclusion zone. Floodway/floodplain rules and permit consequences require the actual jurisdiction and proposal.

## Evidence required to close the engineering gaps

The following gates define completion evidence. They are project requirements proposed for this research program, not a claim that every item is mandated by the same source or authority.

| Gap | Evidence needed for closure | Responsibility to identify |
|---|---|---|
| Site and authority | Surveyed road/shoulder profile, property/ROW and permitted staging limits, road owner, utilities, support and approach footprints; applicable regulatory classification | Site survey, property/road authority, responsible engineer |
| Design criteria | Accepted clearance and overlay basis, accessibility provision, load combinations, seismic category, wind and operating envelope; resolution of source conflicts | Responsible road/bridge and access engineers |
| Supports and ground | Pavement/subgrade condition, utility conflicts, bearing/settlement, sliding and three-dimensional overturning; anchors/ballast and ground investigation as applicable | Geotechnical and structural engineers |
| Structure in every state | Checked three-dimensional models for transport, raising, partial extension, asymmetric deployment, connection, occupied service and retraction; member/connection buckling, fatigue and second-order response | Structural/mechanical engineers and independent checker |
| Midspan interface | Stated restraint model, load paths and capacities; alignment and settlement tolerances; positive retention, inspection and tested engagement verification | Structural/mechanical/control engineers |
| Accessible operation | Complete approach geometry and usable width; independent access, elevator reliability where used, queue capacity, rescue/egress and transition details | Access, operations and structural designers |
| Public-road operation | Reviewed traffic-control plan, barriers/exclusion, vehicle strike assessment, utility clearances, emergency routes and stage-by-stage operating procedure | Road authority, traffic and operations designers |
| Demonstrated performance | Controlled prototype evidence for deployment, locking and failure recovery; inspection criteria; independent verification of the analysis and full-scale qualification plan | Test team, responsible engineer and reviewer |
| Benefit at this location | Current crossing counts and destinations, accessibility and route-length comparison, interaction with planned improvements, feasible approach/support space | Transport/site study |

A calculation or map result closes only the question it actually evaluates. The present deliverable closes source identification and preserves query evidence. It does not label the remaining physical-design, test or approval gates complete.
