# Unbounded non-Galois field towers for the fixed Mordell curve

Let C be the smooth projective model of y² = x⁶ − 1, and let k = ℚ(i). For each rational prime p ≡ 5 (mod 12), choose a Gaussian prime π above p with odd real part and even imaginary part, and take its conjugate as the other generator. Signs make no difference to the Gaussian squareclasses. Let W be the squareclass space generated by 3 and these Gaussian primes. Each class has a representative given by a squarefree product of the indicated generators. Its parity ε is the number modulo two of prime factors of norm 5 modulo 24. Equivalently the representative has imaginary part 0 modulo 4 if ε = 0, and 2 modulo 4 if ε = 1. This is additive under multiplication because the real parts are odd. Define Ω by adjoining to k the square roots of all classes in the kernel of ε.

**Theorem 1.** Every finite subfield K of Ω has exactly the two infinities, the points (z,0) for z⁶ = 1 in K, and the points (0,z) for z² = −1 in K. These are at most ten points. The original tower generated over ℚ by i, √3 and all rational √p for p ≡ 5 (mod 12) lies in Ω since π and its conjugate have equal parity. For p ≡ 17 (mod 24) either individual prime has parity zero. If π₁,…,πₙ lie above n distinct such rational primes, k(√π₁,…,√πₙ) has degree 2ⁿ⁺¹ over ℚ and is non-Galois. Their classes are independent by prime valuations, and conjugation moves the class of π₁ outside their span. There are infinitely many eligible primes by Dirichlet's theorem.

**Proof.** Use E given by Y² = X³ − 1 and the map (x,y) ↦ (x²,y). Each finite multiquadratic subextension of Ω over k has its E rank given by the sum of the ranks over k of the character twists
Eδ given by Y² = X³ − δ³. The two rational 2-isogenous models after translation have parameters (a,b) = (3δ,3δ²), (−6δ,−3δ²) in y² = u(u²+au+b). A descent-image representative e requires Z² = eU⁴+aU²V²+(b/e)V⁴ with U,V primitive at each prime. The product of the two image orders equals 4·2ʳ. This is the usual 2-isogeny index formula and works over number fields as well. Only primes of b and units can occur, since valuation outside b is even. The Gaussian integers are a principal ideal domain and their unit squareclasses are 1 and i.

At a Gaussian π dividing δ above p ≡ 5 (mod 12), suppose π divides the squarefree candidate e. Then e, b/e both have valuation one. Upon dividing the homogeneous equation by π and reducing modulo π, both U,V must be units and the discriminant (a²−4b)/π² must be a square. Its squareclass is respectively −3 or 3. Both are nonsquares modulo p. This leaves only e in {1,i,3,3i}. Both images contain 1 and 3 (the latter from the distinguished rational two-torsion class b). To exclude i it suffices to check the two charts (t,1),(1,t) in ℤ[i]/4, where δ equals 1 or −1. In the first model the three coefficients for e=i are (i,3δ,−3iδ²); in the second they are (i,−6δ,3iδ²). Neither chart produces a square for either sign of δ. All sixteen ring elements and all four coefficients/sign cases are checked in tower_replay.py. Consequently the two image orders are exactly two and every twist Eδ has rank zero.

The good reductions of Eδ at the inert Gaussian primes 11 and 23 have residue cardinalities 121 and 529. Over ℱ_{p²} the baseline E has order (p+1)², because cubing is bijective over ℱ_p and its trace there is zero. A quadratic twist has order (p+1)² or (p−1)². Thus the two sets of possible orders are {100,144}, {484,576}. Torsion injects away from the residue characteristic; only 3 can divide its odd part. The polynomial for nonidentity 3-torsion abscissas is 3X(X³−4δ³). The element 4 is not a cube in k, so X = 0 and −δ³ must be a square, forcing the trivial twist since −1 is square in k. Its odd torsion is exactly 3, either by the baseline calculation over ℚ(i) or the isotypic decomposition over ℚ for E and its −1 twist. At odd torsion orders the same character projection as for ranks is valid since powers of two are invertible.

There is no point of order four over Ω. The field contains all cube roots of unity, so automorphism by multiplication of X by a cube root permits reduction to a half of (1,0). Its coordinates satisfy X = 1 ± √3, Y² = 9 ± 6√3. The norm of the latter element from k(√3) to k is −27, a nonsquare in k. In an elementary abelian 2-extension of k containing k(√3), a squareclass of k(√3) that becomes a square must come from a squareclass of k, and hence has square norm. Indeed every relative quadratic subextension is obtained by adjoining a square root from k. This contradiction excludes order four. Thus E(Ω) is generated by E[2] and (0,i), with finite abscissas 0, ζ, −2ζ for ζ³ = 1. Each ζ is a square in k(√3); the squareclass of −2 in k is the unit class i, outside W by independence of the odd prime valuations. Taking fibres of x² = X proves the theorem.

## Two towers involving 2

Put P = {p prime, p ≡ 5 (mod 12)}. Set N₊ = ℚ(√2,√−3,√p : p∈P) and N₋ = ℚ(√−2,√−3,√p : p∈P).

**Theorem 2.** For K⊂N₊ finite, C(K) consists of the two infinities and (z,0) for z∈μ₆(K). For K⊂N₋ finite the same list holds with these additional points whenever t=√−2/2 belongs to K:
(ut, v·3t/2) for u∈μ₆(K), v∈{−1,1}. In particular every subfield between ℚ(√−2,√−3) and N₋ has exactly twenty points.

**Proof.** Write m for a product of distinct primes from P. In addition to the odd twists proved previously it suffices for E to be rank zero over ℚ for d = 2m and −6m. Put n=d/2, so n≡1 (mod 4). The same prime test excludes divisors of m from isogeny images. The first image is positive at the real place. Besides {1,3} its only candidate modulo the known class is e=2. Its quartic is 2(U⁴+3nU²V²+3n²V⁴). The bracket is odd for any primitive pair, an obstruction. On the dual side multiply by the known class −3 to leave representatives 1,−1,2,−2. In the middle two cases e≡−1 (mod 3); reduction forces 3|U and the last term then has valuation 1+2v₃(d), strictly less than the other two terms. For e=−2 the quartic is −2(U⁴+6nU²V²−3n²V⁴). The bracket is odd for opposite parity, and 4 modulo 8 if both inputs are odd. These give an odd valuation in either case. Thus the rank is zero, giving rank-zero E on N₊ by characters. On N₋ use E₊:Y²=X³+1 and its quotient (−1/x²,y/x³); the character twists with even d correspond to E with −d of the already treated form. Over ℚ the only possible odd rational torsion of Y²=X³−d³ is order 3 on d=−1, by the 11,23 bounds and the same 3-division argument. The base curve at d=−1 has order 6. There is no 4-torsion on either tower since the ordinate of a half of (±1,0) generates a mixed signature field, impossible inside an abelian extension of ℚ. Neither tower contains i by squareclass independence. N₊ thus gives just the branch points and infinities. N₋ has E₊[2] and the rational order-three generator; finite target abscissas are 0, −ζ, 2ζ (ζ³=1). The last case gives x⁶=−1/8,y²=−9/8, yielding exactly the stated extra points. Restriction to subfields is exact because their ordinate generates ℚ(√−2).

## Full point lists for non-Galois quartic fields with unrestricted dyadic parity

**Theorem 3.** Let K=k(√d) be quadratic over k=ℚ(i). Suppose that every prime ideal at which d has odd valuation either divides 6 or has residue cardinality congruent to 2 modulo 3. Then C(K) consists of the six points of C(k), except when K=k(√3), where there are ten, or K=k(√2), where there are eighteen. This allows single π of norm 5 modulo 24 as well as 17 modulo 24. For K=k(√3) add the four missing zero-ordinate points. For K=k(√2), put h=√−2. In addition to the six base points use the four sign choices at each of (i,h), (h/2,3h/4), (h,3i).

**Proof.** The earlier exact enumeration of J(k) gives four exceptional quadratic divisor pairs over k(√2), two over k(√3), and twelve over k(√7). Therefore among fields covered by the hypothesis the first two acquire eight and four points respectively with abscissa outside k, and all other such fields acquire none. The field k(√7) is excluded by its odd valuation at the inert Gaussian prime 7 of norm 49. If x∈k and the source ordinate lies outside k, d(x⁶−1) must be square in k. Write x=u/v primitively and F=u⁴+u²v²+v⁴. The gcd of F and u²−v² is supported over 3 since u=±v gives F=3v⁴. At every odd residue prime of cardinality 2 modulo 3 other than 3, F has no projective zero, since a root would give a nontrivial cube root of unity via (u/v)². Also F is a unit at 1+i. Thus every odd valuation of F is at the Gaussian prime 3, and its possible squareclasses are 1,i,3,3i. In both normalized charts over ℤ[i]/4, neither iF nor 3iF is a square, by direct enumeration. The remaining classes j=1,3 give a point with abscissa X=jx² on Y²=X(X²+jX+j²). The rational ranks of this model and its −1 twist are zero for both j. Enumeration of its Gaussian torsion, of orders 8 and 4 respectively, gives only x=0,±i for j=1, and x=0,±1 for j=3. At x=±i the source requires √−2. The explicit points above now account for all allowed abscissas and the exact exceptional-divisor counts.

**Rational companion.** If d is a signed squarefree integer without a prime divisor congruent to 1 modulo 3, the same argument over ℚ leaves only positive squareclasses 1,3 for F, and hence abscissas 0,±1 when rational. The previously classified exceptional nonrational abscissas occur only for d=−2,−3. Thus ℚ(√d) has exactly four points unless d=−1 (six) or d=−2,−3 (eight each). This applies to arbitrary products of the inert primes, including every prime p≡11 (mod 12).

## The next quadratic extension of the entire infinite tower

Let F₀=ℚ(ζ₁₂)=k(√3). Write Ωall for k with the square roots of all of W adjoined, including classes of odd parity. Thus Ωall=Ω(√(1+2i)).

**Theorem 4.** Every point of C(Ωall) has abscissa in Ω. Every additional point beyond the ten in C(Ω) is equivalent to a solution of v²=(1+2i)(x⁶−1) with x,v∈Ω and v nonzero. This is a reduction of the remaining equation over the infinite tower. Existence of such additional solutions is open in this calculation.

**Proof.** Write J=Jac(C). If s is the deck involution of the map to E, the other degree-two elliptic quotient has deck involution equal to the hyperelliptic involution composed with s. The sum of the pushforward–pullback homomorphisms on J is (1+s)+(1−s)=[2]. Both target groups over Ω have exponent six by Theorem 1 (the two curves are isomorphic over k). Hence J(Ω) is contained in J[12]. The curve has good reduction at every prime outside 2,3 by its branch polynomial. The prime-to-residue-characteristic torsion on its Jacobian is unramified at each such prime by the good-reduction implication of the Néron–Ogg–Shafarevich criterion. In contrast any nontrivial finite subextension of Ω/F₀ contains a quadratic radical class with odd valuation at some prime above p≡5 (mod 12). Such a quadratic extension ramifies there, since the valuation remains odd after scalar extension from k to F₀. The only generator in W supported over 2,3 alone is 3, already square over F₀. Thus J(Ω)=J(F₀).

Over F₀ good split reductions at the rational primes 13,37 have Jacobian orders 144 and 2304, bounding the torsion order by 144. In coordinates z=1/(x−1), t=z³y the odd model is t²=(z+1)⁶−z⁶. Starting with all five affine branch-point classes and the classes at (z,t)=(0,1), (−1,i), exact Cantor addition generates 144 distinct reduced classes. Of these 96 have irreducible quadratic support q(z). For its discriminant D∈F₀ the norm to k is a square for 72 of those 96, a necessary condition to split in any elementary 2-extension of k. If s²=Norm(D), the class of D over F₀ is represented by δ=Tr(D)+2s∈k, choosing the sign to avoid zero, since Dδ=(D+s)². Comparing the exact Gaussian prime valuations and the residual unit class of δ against W shows that none of these 72 splits in Ωall. The choice of representative modulo 3 is immaterial since 3∈W. The script tower_divisors.py recomputes every divisor and every test.

If x lies outside Ω, the point and its Ω-conjugate yield a degree-two effective divisor off the hyperelliptic pencil. Such a divisor is unique in its linear equivalence class. Its class lies in J(F₀) after subtracting twice the rational base Weierstrass point, so its support is one of the irreducible F₀-quadratics treated above. This contradiction proves the assertion about x. For x∈Ω, any ordinate lies in Ω or in √(1+2i)·Ω, proving the final equivalence. No nonzero source ordinate in Ω yields a point on the latter twist because 1+2i is nonsquare there.

The new parity class actually gives positive rank. For δ = 1+2i, the point (X,Y) = (1−i,3) lies on Eδ over k, since (1−i)³−(1+2i)³=9. Its exact k-torsion has order two. Thus E acquires positive rank already over k(√(1+2i)), and it keeps positive rank over Ωall. The two direct elliptic quotients become isomorphic over k, so no elliptic quotient of C over Ωall restores the finite-target argument. The 39th-point assignment over ℚ(i,√7) is independent of this parity-odd family.

Good reduction reference: J. S. Milne, *Complex Multiplication*, Corollary 6.11, https://www.jmilne.org/math/CourseNotes/CM.pdf.

**Complete quadratic-extension classification for nonbase abscissas.** For an arbitrary quadratic extension L/Ω, even one whose radicand belongs to no specified small subfield, the number of points of C(L) with abscissa outside Ω is zero except for exactly these five extensions. Here L is obtained by adjoining the square root of the listed radicand.

| Radicand | Number of points with abscissa outside Ω |
|---|---:|
| 2 | 24 |
| 7 | 72 |
| 11 | 48 |
| 13 + 8√3 | 24 |
| 13 − 8√3 | 24 |

For each of the 96 irreducible F₀-quadratics with discriminant D and each displayed radicand c, test whether D/c is square in Ω. Squares already in F₀ are immediate. Otherwise compute the norm and Gaussian representative by the trace identity above, and require that representative to lie in the dyadic kernel of W. This equivalence follows because the only squareclasses of F₀ which become squares in Ω are restrictions of the kernel classes in k; the kernel of restriction from k to F₀ is generated by 3, already a kernel class. The five candidates are nontrivial and pairwise distinct by the same exact test. The 96 supports match them uniquely with multiplicities 12,36,24,12,12, exhausting all possible exceptions. Each pair contributes two distinct points. The two final representatives have norm −23 to k and cannot occur in an exponent-two extension of k. Their product −23 also separates their squareclasses over Ω.

The companion file exceptional-divisors.json gives all 96 Mumford pairs in the odd chart. Array entries are in ascending powers of z=1/(x−1), and each coefficient is a polynomial in w=ζ₁₂ satisfying w⁴−w²+1=0. A point is recovered from u(z)=0 and ordinate t=v(z) by x=1+1/z, y=t/z³. The counts in the table apply only to nonbase abscissas. For every L the remaining points reduce to a single quadratic twist over Ω as in Theorem 4, with the defining radicand of L replacing 1+2i.
