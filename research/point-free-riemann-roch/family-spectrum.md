# A quartic family with three distinct rational elliptic-map degree sequences

Let $e$ be a positive integer with $e\equiv10\pmod{24871}$, put $q=A^2+2B^2$ and $g=A^3-6AB^2$, and take the projective quartic
$$C_e:\quad q^2+Hg+3H^2q+eH^4=0.$$
The [mixed input](nonsplit.json) uses $e=10$, $A=X+2Y$, $B=Y+Z$ and $H=X-Y+Z$. The [program](family_spectrum.py) evaluates the degree formulas below, checks the family identities and reproduces the Frobenius and isogeny certificates.

| Ground field and target | Map degrees and classes, up to translation and target isomorphism |
|---|---|
| Elliptic targets over $\mathbb Q$ | Exactly $2n^2$, $6n^2$ and $12n^2$ for $n\ge1$, with one class in each degree so obtained. |
| Elliptic targets over $\overline{\mathbb Q}$ | Exactly the multiples of two or three. |
| Primitive geometric maps | One class of degree three. In degree $2m$ there are $3\cdot2^{s(m)}$ classes if $m$ has no prime divisor congruent to two modulo three and $9\nmid m$, and zero otherwise. Here $s(m)$ counts the distinct prime divisors of $m$ congruent to one modulo three. |

There are infinitely many geometric source isomorphism classes in this progression. The unique geometric cubic direction has nonconstant target invariant, given by the binary-quartic invariants below. The cubic class is defined over $\mathbb Q$ as a map to a genus-one curve of period and index two. Its first composition with a morphism to a rationally pointed elliptic curve has degree twelve. The degree-six map in the table is already geometrically primitive.

## Three maps and a geometric decomposition

In $H=1$ write $a=A/H$, $b=B/H$, $q=a^2+2b^2$, $g=a^3-6ab^2$. The source is smooth by the verified special fibre at 11. It is real-point-free: $g^2+2b^2(3a^2-2b^2)^2=q^3$. In homogeneous coordinates this bounds $|Hg|$ by $|H|q^{3/2}$, whereas $q^2-|H|q^{3/2}+3H^2q$ is nonnegative and vanishes only if $q=0$ or both terms vanish. The additional positive $eH^4$ excludes every nonzero real vector.

Rotation of $(a,b)$ by
$$\rho(a,b)=\left(\frac{-a+\sqrt6\,b}{2},-\frac b2-\frac{\sqrt6\,a}{4}\right)$$
has order three and preserves $q,g$. Its Galois conjugate is its inverse. The involution $\tau:(a,b)\mapsto(a,-b)$ conjugates $\rho$ to $\rho^{-1}$. Their elliptic quotients can be recovered from the plane equation by the [bitangent method](cyclic-quotients.md) and ordinary completion of the square.

The cyclic quotient $\pi:C_e\to T_e$ has
$$t=q,\quad y=2b(3a^2-2b^2),\qquad T_e:\ y^2=-2(t^2+3t+e)^2+2t^3.$$
The degree-three construction gives a finite map between the smooth projective models. Every real fibre of an odd-degree map would have a real point (count geometric points with multiplicities), so $T_e(\mathbb R)$ is empty. Its double cover of the line gives a rational effective divisor of degree two; hence its period and index are two. The Jacobian is $E_e:Y^2=X^3-27 I X-27 J$, where
$$I=4(16e^2-54e+81),\qquad J=-8(128e^3-675e^2+1458e-1458).$$
For example $j(E_{10})=23767139536/1207375$. These are the ordinary binary-quartic invariants with $4I^3-J^2=1728e^3(256e^2-747e+1188)$. The rational degree-four morphism $T_e\to E_e$ is $P\mapsto[2P-D]$ for a rational degree-two divisor $D$. Explicit covariant formulas use $F$ for the homogenized quartic on the right above, $K=F_{tt}F_{zz}-F_{tz}^2$, and $G=F_tK_z-F_zK_t$. On $z=1$ the coordinates are $X=-K/(4F)$, $Y=3G/(32y^3)$, as in Part XXVIII, §132.

Completion of the square in $b^2$ gives the degree-two quotient $\phi:C_e\to R_e$ by $\tau$:
$$R_e:\ Y^2=X^3+261X+1494-1024e,\qquad X=3-16a,\quad Y=16(2q-3a+3).$$
In particular the map has a rationally pointed elliptic target even though its source has no real point. Its field is generated by $a,b^2$.

A third rational map is obtained from $\phi\rho-\phi\rho^{-1}$ using the group law on $R_e$. Conjugation over $\mathbb Q(\sqrt6)$ negates the difference. It descends to the quadratic twist $R_e^{(6)}$ with coefficients $36\cdot261$ and $216(1494-1024e)$. For $u=(4q+3a+6)/b$, $x_0=3+8a$, its coordinates are
$$X_6=u^2-12x_0,\qquad Y_6=864b+u(X_6-6x_0).$$
The program verifies the twist equation and the elliptic subtraction formula over the family function field. Rational maps from a smooth projective curve to these proper smooth targets extend uniquely at the apparent poles.

## The four-prime certificate

Since $24871=7\cdot11\cdot17\cdot19$, the following good-reduction traces hold throughout the progression.

| Curve | 7 | 11 | 17 | 19 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| $E_e$ | 5 | 4 | −2 | 5 |
| $R_e$ | −2 | 5 | 5 | −4 |

Direct projective fibre counts on the plane quartic agree with the product of the three elliptic numerators over the first three extension degrees at each of these four primes. They are recomputed by solving the source equation as a quadratic in $b^2$, followed by a square-root test and the points at infinity.

At 7 both reductions are ordinary. Their Frobenius quadratic fields are $\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-3})$ and $\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-6})$. Therefore $E_e$ and $R_e$ are geometrically nonisogenous: a geometric isogeny would persist between the good reductions over a finite extension, whereas these ordinary endomorphism fields are distinct. At 11 their respective ordinary fields are $\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-7})$ and $\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-19})$. Comparing each curve's ordinary fields at 7 and 11 rules out complex multiplication in characteristic zero. Furthermore $(6/7)=-1$ and $a_7(R_e)=-2$, so $R_e$ and $R_e^{(6)}$ are distinct rational isogeny classes.

For each possible prime degree $\ell\in\{2,3,5,7,11,13,17,19,37,43,67,163\}$ and each of the two rows, at least one $p\ne\ell$ has no root of $x^2-a_p x+p$ modulo $\ell$. The program tests every residue. A Galois-stable line in $E[\ell]$ would give such a root at all good $p\ne\ell$. The prime-degree theorem therefore excludes every rational cyclic prime isogeny for both curves. Twisting multiplies each Galois matrix by a scalar and preserves its stable lines, so the same conclusion holds for $R_e^{(6)}$. More generally any nontrivial Galois-stable finite subgroup has some $\ell$-torsion and must contain all of $E[\ell]$. After factoring out multiplication by $\ell$, induction shows that every rational isogeny from one of the three is multiplication by an integer followed by a target isomorphism. [Mazur, *Rational isogenies of prime degree*](https://www.math.columbia.edu/~goldfeld/Mazur-Goldfeld1978.pdf)

## Why the degrees exhaust all maps

Use the homomorphisms of Jacobians induced by the maps. Translations disappear in these homomorphisms. Write $v_1=\phi$, $v_2=\phi\rho$, $v_3=\phi\rho^{-1}$ for their homomorphism parts over $\overline{\mathbb Q}$. Their sum is rotation-invariant and factors through the cyclic quotient up to isogeny. To see this directly on Jacobians, the norm operator $1+\rho_*+\rho_*^2$ is $\pi^*\pi_*$. Since $E_e$ and $R_e$ are geometrically nonisogenous, $v_1+v_2+v_3=0$. Each has degree two and $v_1+v_2=-v_3$ has degree two. The elliptic degree form, obtained from $v v^\dagger=[\deg(v)]$ under the canonical polarizations, is consequently
$$\deg(av_1+bv_2)=2(a^2-ab+b^2).$$
In particular the difference $v_2-v_3=v_1+2v_2$ has degree six. The three exhibited directions give nonzero maps from the three-dimensional Jacobian to $E_e,R_e,R_e^{(6)}$, which are pairwise nonisogenous over $\mathbb Q$. Complete reducibility now gives its full rational isogeny decomposition. Geometrically the decomposition is $E_e\times R_e^2$, up to isogeny. As $R_e$ has no CM, every geometric elliptic direction in the repeated factor is a rational slope $av_1+bv_2$.

For coprime $a,b$ this slope map has connected kernel on the Jacobian. Indeed $v_1v_1^\dagger=v_2v_2^\dagger=[2]$ and $v_1v_2^\dagger=v_2v_1^\dagger=[-1]$, since the endomorphism ring is $\mathbb Z$ and the cross coefficient of the degree form is $-2$. Composing $v=av_1+bv_2$ with the two pullbacks shows that its image in integral first homology contains $h H_1(R_e,\mathbb Z)$, with $h=\gcd(2a-b,2b-a)\mid3$. For $h=1$ this is already surjectivity. For $h=3$, $(a,b)\equiv a(1,2)\pmod3$ and $a$ is a unit modulo three. The map $v_1+2v_2$ is the rational degree-six map to the twist, after extending scalars. If its Jacobian kernel were disconnected, its rational optimal quotient would give a nontrivial isogeny to the twist of degree dividing six. By the rational isogeny certificate every such isogeny has square degree; the only square dividing six is one. Thus this comparison map surjects on integral homology. Reducing modulo three shows that $v$ too is surjective modulo three, and the bound by $h$ proves integral surjectivity. This certifies the optimal degree for every geometric slope. The cubic map is primitive because a nontrivial isogeny factor of a degree-three curve map would leave a degree-one map from a genus-three curve to a genus-one curve.

On the rational reflection and twist directions, the degree-two and degree-six maps are defined over $\mathbb Q$ and are geometrically optimal. Every further rational elliptic map on either direction is multiplication by an integer up to target isomorphism and translation. On the cubic direction, all maps factor geometrically through $T_e$ by primitivity. A rational map on this direction factors over $\mathbb Q$ through the rationally defined quotient. A map $T_e\to E_e$ inducing $[k]$ on Jacobians exists precisely when $k[T_e]=0$ in $H^1(\mathbb Q,E_e)$: over the algebraic closure choose an origin, and changing it by a torsor cocycle changes the proposed map by $k$ times that cocycle. The period is two, and the rational degree-four map realizes $k=2$. Thus the realized degrees from $C_e$ along that direction are exactly $12n^2$. The three degree sequences are disjoint and each target contributes one class up to translation and isomorphism.

Finally, a primitive slope of norm $m=a^2-ab+b^2$ is counted up to simultaneous negation, since the target's automorphisms fixing an origin are $\{\pm1\}$. In the Euclidean Eisenstein integers, an inert prime dividing a primitive norm would divide both coefficients. The ramified prime can occur in the norm only to exponent one. For every split prime dividing $m$, its entire exponent is assigned to one of the two conjugate prime factors, and multiplication by the six units gives three choices after sign. This proves the primitive count in the table. For each primitive direction of degree $k\mid d$, the number of degree-$d$ classes above it is $\sigma_1(d/k)$, the number of index-$d/k$ sublattices of a rank-two lattice. Distinct directions have distinct connected kernels. The program sums those contributions and independently counts primitive norm vectors through norm 100.
