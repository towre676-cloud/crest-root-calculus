# Cyclic quotients from source coefficients

Let $K\ge2$ and $C$ be the smooth projective model of $y^K=P(x)$ over $\mathbb Q$. Write $P=S^K F$ with $S$ monic, keeping the leading unit in $F$, and with finite root multiplicities in $\{1,\ldots,K-1\}$ for $F$. Assume $n=\deg F\ge2$ and $\gcd(K,n)=1$. The unique point above infinity is rational and totally ramified. Its pole orders for $x$ and $y/S$ are $K$ and $n$. In particular the source is geometrically integral. Unless needed for the input formula, write $y_0=y/S$.

The target is the smooth projective model of $z^k=G(u)$, where $k\ge2$, $G$ is squarefree of degree $m$, $\gcd(k,m)=1$, and the target has positive genus. The source infinity must be the whole fibre above target infinity. The main theorem recovers all rational maps of total degree at least two that intertwine the two cyclic actions, allowing any surjective character. Maps are identified by pointed isomorphisms that preserve the specified target action. Geometric support counts are computed for the normalized incidence of each character and degree, while formulas are exported for the rational solutions.

## Reduce every equivariant character to a coefficient identity

Over a field containing the required roots of unity, the cyclic action on $C$ sends $y_0$ to $\zeta_K y_0$, and the target action sends $z$ to $\zeta_k z$. An equivariant map has invariant horizontal pullback and a vertical pullback of weight $a$. The target action must be generated by the image of the source action. Otherwise the map descends, after quotienting the target by that image, to a map from the projective line to $v^{k/r}=G(u)$ with $r<k$ the size of the image. This latter curve has positive genus since $\gcd(k,m)=1$ and the target has positive genus. A nonconstant map from the projective line to it is impossible. Consequently $k\mid K$, and the weight is $a=(K/k)j$ with $1\le j<k$ and $\gcd(j,k)=1$.

The intermediate function field generated by $x$ and $y_0^{K/k}$ has exponent $k$ and index $K/k$ in the source. Replacing its vertical generator by its $j$th power and dividing by a monic polynomial $D$ gives
$$
 F^j=D^k B,\qquad w=\frac{y_0^{(K/k)j}}{D(x)},\qquad w^k=B(x).
$$
Here $B$ has finite multiplicities less than $k$ and need not be squarefree. Its degree is coprime to $k$ since $\deg B\equiv j\deg F\pmod k$. At a finite zero of $B$ of multiplicity $b<k$, put $g=\gcd(k,b)$. The orders of $x-\alpha$ and $w$ on the normalized intermediate curve are $k/g$ and $b/g$. Hence a function $wR(x)$ has no pole there only if $R$ has no pole. The same conclusion holds at unbranched points. The unique-infinity condition therefore gives
$$
 u=U(x),\qquad z=wV(x),\qquad G(U)=B V^k
$$
with polynomials over $\mathbb Q$. The argument also applies after base change. Normalize by affine changes of $u$ and scaling of $z$ so that $U,V$ are monic and $U(0)=0$. These are all pointed equivariant changes between the stated target models, since the invariant functions with pole order at most $k$ are spanned by $1,u$, and the weight-one functions with pole order at most $m$ are spanned by $z$. If $d=\deg U$, the intermediate curve map has degree $d$ by comparing degrees over $\mathbb Q(U)$. The original map has total degree $dK/k$. The exported vertical formula uses the original $y$ and denominator $S^{(K/k)j}D$.

## Degree bounds and the complementary recurrence

Put $N=\deg B$, $L=\operatorname{lc}B$ and $e=\deg V$. The identity gives $md=N+ke$ and $\operatorname{lc}G=L$. Since $G'(U)$ is coprime to $V$, differentiating gives $V^{k-1}\mid U'$. Write $U'=dV^{k-1}W$ with $W$ monic and $w_0=\deg W=d-1-(k-1)e$. This yields
$$
 ((k-1)m-k)d\le (k-1)N-k.
$$
The coefficient of $d$ is $2g_{\rm target}-1>0$. We apply the tighter Riemann–Hurwitz bound
$$
 (2g_{\rm target}-1)d\le 2g_{\rm intermediate}-1,
$$
because the specified fibre already contributes $d-1$ ramification. With $B=\lambda\prod_i B_i^i$ its squarefree multiplicity decomposition, the intermediate genus is given by
$$
 2g_{\rm intermediate}=1-k+\sum_i\deg B_i\,\bigl(k-\gcd(k,i)\bigr).
$$
The polynomial inequality also gives $m<N$ when $d>1$. The case $d=1$ can contribute a nontrivial original map when $K>k$. It is permitted only when $B$ is squarefree, and in normalized coordinates it gives $U=x,V=1,G=B$.

Let $t=1/x$ and let $f=t^NB(1/t)/L$, $v=t^eV(1/t)$, $b=t^{w_0}W(1/t)$ and $A=t^dU(1/t)$. Over characteristic zero,
$$
 A\equiv(fv^k)^{1/m}\pmod {t^d},\qquad dA-tA'=d v^{k-1}b.
$$
The first equality follows because all lower outer terms start at order $t^d$. For $1\le i\le e$, let $v_{<i}$ be the already known truncation of $v$. Put
$$
 r_i=[t^i](fv_{<i}^k)^{1/m},\qquad s_i=[t^i](v_{<i}^{\,k-1}b).
$$
The only new contribution to the leading-root equation is $kv_i/m$. The only new contribution to the derivative product is $(k-1)v_i$. Thus
$$
 v_i=\frac{(d-i)r_i-ds_i}{d(k-1)-k(d-i)/m}.
$$
The denominator is positive since $m\ge2$, $k\ge2$, $i\ge1$. The recurrence determines $V$ polynomially from the coefficients of $W$, which determine the candidate $U$ by the first identity. Conversely, monic division of $U'$ by $dV^{k-1}$ recovers exactly $W$. The direct chart uses the coefficients of $V$ and imposes the derivative remainder. Choose the chart with fewer parameters.

Monic division of $BV^k$ by $U$ then yields base-$U$ digits. The positive-degree parts must vanish, and the constants give $G$. This is exact over the parameter ring and commutes with specialization. When $B$ is squarefree, a repeated root of $G$ would force $U-a\mid V^{k-1}$, impossible by degree. Indeed for a root of $U-a$ of multiplicity $s$, a repeated target root gives $k\operatorname{ord}V\ge2s-1$, which implies $(k-1)\operatorname{ord}V\ge s$ by integrality for $k=2$ or $s=1$ and by the inequality for the remaining cases. For mixed $B$ we impose squarefreeness directly, using at most one additional discriminant-inverse variable. Thus the number of factor parameters is $\min(e,d-1-(k-1)e)$, and a stratum with no factor parameters needs only exact univariate arithmetic.

For targets of genus at least two, de Franchis–Severi finiteness applies. For genus one and bounded $d$, the exterior product of the pulls of an oriented integral basis of target cohomology is an integral vector of Hodge area $d$. Bounded area leaves finitely many such vectors. Each fixes the plane and the index of the pullback lattice in its saturation, hence finitely many lattices and induced elliptic tori. Integration of the pulled-back differential from the specified source infinity fixes the pointed map. For either genus case, a pointed isomorphism intertwining maps also intertwines the target actions, by surjectivity of the map. This gives finite geometric support for the normalized incidence. The existing exact finite-algebra solver supplies the rational solutions and complete support count, including exclusions. ## When equivariance follows from the pole order

Write $F=\lambda\prod_i F_i^i$ with $F_i$ monic, mutually coprime and squarefree. The minimal finite-regular function of weight $a$, up to scalar and polynomial factors, is
$$
 q_a = y_0^a\prod_i F_i^{-\lfloor ai/K\rfloor},\qquad
 \lambda_a = \sum_i\deg F_i\, (ai\bmod K).
$$
Here $\lambda_a$ is its pole order at source infinity. The finite-order calculation above proves that the entire finite-regular eigenspace is $q_a\mathbb Q[x]$. Pole orders of different weights are distinct modulo $K$, since $\gcd(K,n)=1$, so the highest poles cannot cancel. This is the pole decomposition underlying the classical one-infinity Kummer semigroup. [Mendoza, Theorem 3.2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.09729)

Put $\lambda_{\min}=\min_{1\le a<K}\lambda_a$. For any pointed map of total degree $D_0$ to a target as above, the horizontal pullback has pole order $kD_0$. If $kD_0<\lambda_{\min}$, it lies in $\mathbb Q[x]$. Its vertical pullback has $k$th power in $\mathbb Q(x)$, so the ratio between any cyclic transform and itself is a constant $k$th root of unity. Thus it is an eigenfunction. The same lower-quotient argument forces a surjective character. In this range the search therefore covers **all** maps with the specified infinity fibre to the indicated class of target models, even if equivariance was never assumed. A nondividing target exponent is excluded in this range. For squarefree $F$, $\lambda_{\min}=\deg F$. The bound is strict and makes no claim past equality.

## Scope and antecedents

The target radicand is squarefree. The source can have mixed branch multiplicities, and its degree after removing powers must be coprime to its exponent. Source-only searches for general infinity fibres or for maps beyond the automatic range that do not respect the cyclic actions remain open in this implementation. The proof bounds parameters and degrees, with no polynomial bit-complexity assertion.

The constant-multiplier method is classical polynomial decomposition. Kozen, Landau and Zippel also give a resultant framework for decomposing algebraic functions, and Mendoza computes the mixed-exponent Kummer pole semigroup. These precedents cover key ingredients and weaken any broad novelty claim. The narrower candidate is the displayed complementary-factor recurrence, its degree-by-degree specialization, and the resulting explicit source-only search. The comparisons here establish exact recovery and measured performance on the stated inputs. Literature priority for that recurrence remains open.
