# Quotient counts as Hecke walks to the product locus

The [opposite-secant theorem](../opposite-secant-spectra/README.md) gives a direct proof for all degrees prime to three, independently of the finite Sturm checks below. The [discriminant −19 equations](../twin-curves/README.md) give quartic-field models for the recognized pair.\n\n**Status.**
- **Proved:** the product formula (from the $T(p^2)$ relation and commutativity of the Hecke operators), and its corollaries.
- **Exactly verified:** 2234 count entries at 8 levels; observed fractions at 36 levels, every non-product pair.
- **Not attempted to completion:** the full 69-level sweep. Levels with closure above roughly 200 classes run 5 to 15 minutes each, and those not listed below did not finish in this session.

## 1. The product formula

Let $p$ be a good prime. On the $p$-neighbour closure the theta series satisfy
$$r(p^2n)+\Big(\tfrac{Dn}{p}\Big)r(n)+p\,r(n/p^2)=(A_pr)(n).$$
Taking $n=m'^2$ with $p\nmid m'$, the middle symbol equals $(\tfrac Dp)\ne0$. Taking $n=p^{2k}m'^2$ with $k\ge1$, it vanishes. With $v(m):=(r_x(m^2))_x$, this gives
$$v(m)=\prod_{p^k\Vert m}Q_{p,k}(A_p)\,v_0,\qquad Q_{p,0}=1,\quad Q_{p,1}=A_p-\Big(\tfrac Dp\Big),\quad Q_{p,k+1}=A_pQ_{p,k}-p\,Q_{p,k-1},$$
for every $m$ coprime to the level. Here $v_0=(r_x(1))_x$.

`product_formula.py` verifies the formula entry by entry against `qfrep` at 8 levels: 2234 entries, every composite $m\le1001$ built from three good primes, no failure.

**The product locus.** At every level, $r_x(1)\in\{0,2\}$. Since $r_x(1)$ counts degree-1 elliptic subvarieties, $v_0=2\cdot\mathbf 1_R$, where $R$ is the set of product classes in the closure. $R$ contains no gluing class.

**Corollary 1 (geometric reading).** The number of degree-$m$ elliptic quotients of a genus-two Jacobian $J$ with $m$ coprime to the level is the weighted number of Hecke correspondences of degree $m$ from $J$ to principally polarised product surfaces in its genus. Coprime-degree injectivity is therefore exactly the statement that these walk profiles to the product locus separate the gluing classes.

**Corollary 2 (observed space).** Hecke operators preserve the genus. Hence, within a genus $g$, the space observed by coprime degrees is the cyclic Hecke module $O_g=\mathbf H\cdot\mathbf 1_{R_g}$, generated by the product classes of that genus. The hidden fraction of a pair difference is $1-\|P_{O_g}(\delta_i/m_i-\delta_j/m_j)\|^2/(1/m_i+1/m_j)$, in the mass inner product.

No representation numbers beyond norm 1 are needed. This makes exact sweeps far cheaper than theta-coefficient methods. Using finitely many primes gives a submodule of $O_g$, so every computed fraction is a rigorous lower bound.

**Replay of the parallel census.** `observed_fraction.py` reproduces the minimal gluing-pair fraction $61/120$ at $(-8,13)$. It also confirms $\dim O_{\rm split}=\dim O_{\rm nonsplit}=h_g-\dim V_g$ at all 12 levels of `../inverse-spectrum`.

One pitfall is recorded so nobody repeats it: projecting onto the joint module $\mathbf H\cdot\mathbf 1_R$ across both genera, instead of its per-genus restrictions, undercounts the observed fraction. A shared eigensystem contributes one joint direction, but one direction per genus.

## 3. Coprime-isospectral twins at $\ell=3$

At $(D,\ell)=(-19,3)$, $(-43,3)$, $(-67,3)$ and $(-163,3)$ the split genus contains pairs of non-product classes with observed fraction exactly 0 (`collisions.py`). All gluing classes at these levels were enumerated, and none lies in a colliding pair. This is consistent with the parallel census, which proves gluing injectivity there.

`collision_counts.py` decides each pair exactly. Each of the 31 pairs agrees at every square $m^2$ with $\gcd(m,6\ell|D|)=1$; this is proved by checking up to the Sturm bound of the $t=1$ Shimura lift (240, 528, 816 and 1968). Each pair first differs at a square whose root is divisible by $3=\ell$: one of $3,6,9,12,21$. The full theta series differ, so the forms are not isometric.

The numbers of twin pairs are $1,4,7,19$. These equal $h_{\rm split}-h_{\rm nonsplit}=7-6,\ 13-9,\ 19-12,\ 43-24$ exactly. So at $\ell=3$ the entire forced kernel of the parallel branch is spanned by two-term differences $e_x-e_y$ between non-gluing Jacobians.

**What this shows about the inverse problem.**
- Within the full CM isogeny class, coprime quotient degrees do **not** determine the principally polarised surface. Genuine genus-two Jacobians exist whose elliptic-quotient counts agree at every degree prime to $6\ell|D|$ and first differ at a degree divisible by $\ell$.
- The parallel branch shows that the forced relations have small projector diagonals at large $\ell$; at $\ell=3$ they realise outright collisions. The injectivity of coprime degrees on nonscalar gluings is therefore a property of the gluing family, not of the genus.
- A uniform proof must use the gluing structure. Spectral delocalisation of the forced and residual kernels on the whole genus is not enough at small $\ell$.

The twins are not related by the adjoint form, by a scaled index-3 sublattice, or by a Kneser 3-neighbour step; all three were tested on every pair. The natural candidate is the plane-sum relation of the parallel branch, which at $\ell=3$ involves exactly two classes.
