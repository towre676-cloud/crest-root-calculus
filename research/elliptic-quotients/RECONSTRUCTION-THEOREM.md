# Reconstruction certificates for genus two

The package implements three verifiers for geometric elliptic subcovers of a genus-two curve. The [Frobenius verifier](exact-completeness/README.md) can exclude all elliptic maps at one absolutely simple good reduction. The [QM verifier](qm-certificate/README.md) does so on a specific quaternionic-multiplication example by checking two global correspondences. The [Néron–Severi verifier](ns-certificates/README.md) certifies the integral lattice and polarization pairing in twelve split examples. This covers isomorphic non-CM factors, geometrically distinct factors that are nonisogenous or isogenous, and two CM examples.

## Exact finite certificates

For the Frobenius rule an irreducible characteristic polynomial of the \(240\)-th power at a good prime implies that all Frobenius powers have irreducible characteristic polynomial. Any elliptic subvariety would survive at a good reduction, so one such prime proves absence in every degree.

For the QM rule verify explicitly that two endomorphisms generate a division quaternion algebra \(B\) over \(\mathbb Q\). A split abelian surface has a commutative endomorphism algebra, \(M_2(\mathbb Q)\), or \(M_2(K)\) for an imaginary quadratic field \(K\). The first two are incompatible with \(B\). In the third case the two geometric elliptic factors have the same CM field, and at ordinary reductions their geometric Frobenius fields must be \(K\). Two distinct ordinary fields exclude this. This distinction via two Frobenius fields is part of [Lombardo, Algorithm 4.5 and Proposition 4.6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1610.09674). In the example the two identities in the function field of \(C\) are the global data that distinguish quaternionic multiplication from a coincidental split reduction.

For presence, exact maps give Rosati-symmetric endomorphisms and integral divisor classes. Use a basis of their span at the certified Néron–Severi rank. Clear denominators and use prime-to-characteristic torsion to saturate. The resulting integral form and polarization pairing determine the degree spectrum by [Kani's correspondence](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jnt.2018.05.011). The exact verifier replays the construction of the Gram matrix, the rank check and the saturation, in addition to recomputing the spectrum. Two isogenous targets with distinct \(j\)-invariants can use a verified isogeny to form additional classes. The CM examples use an additional sum involving the CM automorphism.

## Certificate existence and search termination

There is an important distinction between an effective algebraic procedure in principle and a guarantee for this repository's map and correspondence search.

**In principle.** [Lombardo, §3](https://doi.org/10.1090/mcom/3358) gives a terminating endomorphism-ring procedure for an explicitly presented abelian variety over a number field and discusses passage to the geometric ring. This supplies a general algebraic route to the Rosati-symmetric lattice of a genus-two Jacobian. Intersection numbers can be computed from the action of the endomorphisms and the principal polarization. The existence of a nonzero rational isotropic vector is decidable for this integral quadratic form. In the positive case choose its sign using the polarization and take its primitive integral representative. Kani's correspondence then gives an elliptic subvariety. For any bound \(X\), the positive definite form \((D\cdot\theta)^2-D^2\) makes enumeration finite. A class represented by a symmetric endomorphism \(\alpha_D\) with square zero as an intersection number gives its elliptic subvariety as the connected component of \(\ker\alpha_D\). Its polarized adjoint composed with the Abel–Jacobi map constructs the corresponding optimal map over the algebraic closure.

**This implementation.** Its exact checks prove each reported output. Its period recognizer uses bounded numerical proposals, and the stored torsion checks use specified finite fields. The existence of algebraic maps or endomorphisms by itself does not prove that these numerical routines will recover them at some higher precision. A direct termination proof would have to supply an exhaustive algebraic search or prove eventual recognition and unbounded torsion coverage. The coefficient field of an explicit based map may also require a finite extension of the field generated by its differential ratio. The general coefficient recognizer here uses that ratio to propose a field and can abstain.

There are strong existence results for reductions. For geometrically simple abelian surfaces with commutative geometric endomorphism algebra the absolute-simplicity route is guaranteed to find primes in the density theorem, after the appropriate finite extension. In the QM case the global algebra is needed, and Lombardo already proves termination of an exclusion test for a CM square once QM has been certified. See [Zywina](https://doi.org/10.1093/imrn/rnt113) and [Lombardo](https://doi.org/10.1090/mcom/3358). These results support an effective framework and establish the relevant prior art. They do not establish a priority claim for a universal solver here.

## Certified bounded period searches

The [ball-arithmetic replay](rigorous-search/README.md) checks exhaustive bounded searches against nine independently recovered geometric endomorphism and Néron–Severi lattices. Exact maps and correspondences identify integral homology matrices in the certified period basis. A checked rank bound and an integral double kernel then give the full saturated ring. In the split cases this also verifies the torsion-certified basis by an explicit unimodular change of coordinates. The ambient search checks the elliptic candidates one plane at a time and rejects every extra endomorphism candidate by an exact or ball-certified inequality. For the quaternionic model it recovers the maximal order of reduced discriminant 6 and a modulo-9 obstruction to any rational isotropic class. The search certificates have explicit degree and trace bounds and the complete-ring calculation uses the algebraic inputs and rank witnesses.

## Replay

The Néron–Severi study contains a full replay for its twelve inputs and an exact degree-slice enumerator with no hard-coded degree cap. For the two CM lattices each spectrum has 11,574 classes across 94 degrees through 150. The independent numerical reference goes through degree 40. The QM replay starts from the actual rational functions of the two correspondences over \(\mathbb Q(\zeta_8)\) and checks their membership, tangent action, Hilbert symbols and Frobenius fields. The [period study](period-reconstruction/README.md) also has a coefficient-recognition test over \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt[3]{2})\) obtained by changing the target coordinates of a known map. This tests the field arithmetic and recognition. Full presence certification on a curve requiring a field beyond the Gaussian cases has yet to be demonstrated.
