# Eight geometric maps and two rational maps in degree two

Consider the smooth projective curves

$$C:\quad y^2=(x^2-1)(x^2-2)(x^2-3)(x^2-4),$$

$$D:\quad v^2=u(u-1)(u-2)(u-3)(u-4).$$

Their genera are three and two. Every nonconstant map $C\to D$ has degree two. There are exactly **eight geometric maps** to this specified target and exactly **two rational maps**,

$$u=x^2,\qquad v=\pm xy.$$

The square identity follows immediately from the displayed equations. The infinity fibre has two points, so this example exercises the complete rational-function theorem beyond the singleton-infinity class. The [independent verifier](unramified_example.py) establishes exhaustion through the automorphism groups. [Exact receipt](unramified-example.json)

## Exhausting the degree-two deck transformations

Riemann–Hurwitz gives $d\le(g_C-1)/(g_D-1)=2$. A degree-one map of smooth projective curves is an isomorphism and would preserve genus, so it cannot occur. Every degree-two function-field extension in characteristic zero is Galois. Its unique nontrivial deck transformation is an involution on the source. Thus every map under consideration arises as a genus-two involution quotient followed by an isomorphism with the specified target. [Riemann–Hurwitz, Stacks Project 0C1B](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0C1B)

The geometric branch set of $C$ on the $x$-line is

$$\{-2,-1,1,2,-\sqrt2,\sqrt2,-\sqrt3,\sqrt3\}.$$

Every automorphism of the hyperelliptic curve induces a projective transformation preserving this set, with kernel generated by the hyperelliptic involution. Such a transformation is determined by its action on three points. For rational automorphisms, the images of $(-2,-1,1)$ must lie among the four rational branch points. The verifier checks all $4\cdot3\cdot2=24$ choices. For geometric automorphisms, their images can be any distinct three of the eight branches. The verifier checks all $8\cdot7\cdot6=336$ choices exactly in $\mathbb Q(\sqrt2,\sqrt3)$. Every possible geometric transformation belongs to this field because its three source coordinates are rational and its three image coordinates lie in that field.

Both searches return only $x$ and $-x$. Each has the two vertical lifts $y\mapsto\pm y$. Consequently the entire geometric automorphism group is the Klein-four group, and all its elements are rational. The three involutions have the following fixed-point and quotient counts.

| Involution | Geometric fixed points | Quotient genus |
|---|---:|---:|
| $(x,y)\mapsto(x,-y)$ | 8 | 0 |
| $(x,y)\mapsto(-x,y)$ | 4 | 1 |
| $(x,y)\mapsto(-x,-y)$ | 0 | 2 |

The middle row fixes the two points over $x=0$ and both points at infinity. For the last row the two points over zero are exchanged, since $F(0)=24\ne0$. In the infinity coordinates $t=1/x$ and $Y=y/x^4$, it sends $(t,Y)$ to $(-t,-Y)$ and exchanges the two infinity points $Y=\pm1$. Riemann–Hurwitz then determines the quotient genera. The genus-two deck involution is unique, over the rationals and over an algebraic closure.

Its invariant functions $u=x^2$ and $v=xy$ satisfy the equation of $D$. They generate the invariant field because adjoining $x$ with $x^2=u$ recovers $y=v/x$. This proves that the displayed degree-two map realizes that unique quotient.

## Identifying the maps to the fixed target

The target branch set is $\{0,1,2,3,4,\infty\}$. All its elements are rational, so every geometric projective symmetry is represented by a rational matrix. The verifier tests the $6\cdot5\cdot4=120$ possible images of $(0,1,\infty)$. Exactly four survive. For $A=au+b$, $B=cu+d$, the last column gives $\lambda$ in

$$B^6G(A/B)=\lambda G(u).$$

| Projective transformation | $\lambda$ in the displayed coordinates |
|---|---:|
| $u$ | $1$ |
| $4-u$ | $-1$ |
| $(1-u)/(1-u/2)$ | $1/8$ |
| $(3-u)/(1-u/2)$ | $-1/8$ |

Each has exactly two geometric lifts, with vertical coordinate $\pm\sqrt\lambda\,v/B^3$. Only the first has rational lifts. The target therefore has eight geometric automorphisms and two rational automorphisms. Composing them with the unique source quotient gives exactly eight geometric maps and the two rational maps stated above. Distinct target automorphisms give distinct maps because the quotient is surjective.

## The same cover in an odd-degree source model

Put $x=(z+1)/z$ and $Y=z^4y$. The source becomes

$$Y^2=F_{\rm odd}(z)=\prod_{j=1}^4\big((z+1)^2-jz^2\big).$$

Its degree is seven because the factor with $j=1$ is linear. This rational coordinate change identifies its smooth projective curve with $C$. The cover is now

$$u=\frac{(z+1)^2}{z^2},\qquad
v=\pm\frac{Y(z+1)}{z^5}.$$

For the general compiler take $A=(z+1)^2$, $B=z^2$, and $C_0=z(z+1)$. Then $v=YC_0/B^3$ and

$$B\,G_{\rm hom}(A,B)=F_{\rm odd}(z)C_0^2.$$

The verifier checks this identity and squarefreeness of the transformed degree-seven source exactly. The rational isomorphism transfers both exhaustive map counts to this model.
