# Cyclic triple quotients from rational bitangents

The [algorithm](cyclic_quotients.py) takes the homogeneous equation of any smooth rational plane quartic. It lists every map to a genus-one curve over $\mathbb Q$ whose geometric function-field extension is cyclic of degree three, up to target isomorphism. For every class it gives a target equation, two generating functions, and the quadratic field over which the deck transformations are defined. Translation or a change of coordinates on the target gives an equivalent class. The construction also applies to a rationally stable cyclic class whose individual deck transformations are conjugate. The [general cubic atlas](../README.md) covers the additional non-Galois classes.

## Exhausting the rational lines

A genus-three cyclic triple cover of a genus-one curve has two distinct fixed points of its order-three group, by Riemann–Hurwitz. Every automorphism of a smooth plane quartic acts linearly in its canonical embedding. An order-three projectivity with repeated eigenvalue has a fixed line. At its intersection with the curve, the tangent of the curve must be transverse to that line: otherwise the derivative on the curve is one and a finite-order automorphism in characteristic zero is locally and globally the identity. Thus a fixed line gives at least four distinct fixed points. The projectivity of interest has three distinct eigenpoints; the two on the curve span the line $H=0$, while the third lies off the curve. Normalize eigen-coordinates to $H,L,N$ with eigenvalues $1,\omega,\omega^2$. Since the third eigenpoint is off the source, the quartic contains $H^4$ with nonzero coefficient and is invariant with character one. Its restriction to $H=0$ is proportional to $L^2N^2$. In particular $H$ is an ordinary bitangent, and the two fixed points are its contact divisor. A rationally stable quotient makes this unordered divisor and its line rational.

In the dual chart $H=X+rY+sZ$, restrict the quartic to a binary form
$$aA^4+bA^3B+cA^2B^2+dAB^3+eB^4.$$
When $a\ne0$ it is a scalar times a square exactly if
$$8a^2d=b(4ac-b^2),\qquad 64a^3e=(4ac-b^2)^2.$$
The leading coefficient $a=F(-r,1,0)$ depends only on $r$. The two tests have degree at most three and four in $s$. Their resultant confines $r$ to a univariate rational-root calculation. A test independent of $s$ can serve as the eliminating polynomial directly. A common component over $\mathbb Q(r)$ would give infinitely many bitangents, impossible for a smooth plane quartic in characteristic zero. For each retained $r$ a univariate gcd supplies all possible $s$. At rational roots of $a$ the program checks five binary anchors and uses the gcd of their certificate equations in the remaining parameter. At a square with $a=0$ one has $b=0$, so the displayed polynomial equations remain necessary at every anchor. A nonzero binary quartic cannot vanish at all five anchors, and hence these tests are sufficient. The chart $H=Y+sZ$ and the last line $H=Z$ are processed too. Final factorization verifies each square, including hyperflexes. A hyperflex is excluded from the cubic step because it has only one contact point.

## Recovering the center and the map

Take rational binary coordinates $A,B$ on a bitangent and write
$$F=cq(A,B)^2+Hf_3(A,B)+H^2f_2(A,B)+H^3f_1(A,B)+eH^4,$$
where $q=aA^2+bAB+dB^2$ has distinct roots and $\Delta=b^2-4ad\ne0$. Define the second- and first-order differential operators
$$\mathcal L=d\partial_{AA}-b\partial_{AB}+a\partial_{BB},\qquad J=q_B\partial_A-q_A\partial_B.$$
The center has affine coordinates $(z_0,z_1)$ in the plane $H=1$. Replace $(A,B)$ by $(A+z_0H,B+z_1H)$. In eigen-coordinates, the coefficient of $H$ must be a linear combination of $L^3,N^3$. These span the kernel of $\mathcal L$ on cubics, as one sees in coordinates with $q=LN$. The center is therefore the unique solution of
$$\mathcal L\big(f_3+2cq(z_0q_A+z_1q_B)\big)=0.$$
These are two linear equations over $\mathbb Q$. In split coordinates the map $\mathcal L(q\ell)$ is an invertible scalar multiple of $\ell$ for linear $\ell$, and the gradients of $q$ are independent. Thus there is at most one center per bitangent.

In the centered coordinates the remaining coefficient tests are $f_1=0$ and $f_2=d_0q$. They are also sufficient. The linear transformation on $(A,B)$ given by
$$\tfrac12\left(-I+\sqrt{-3/\Delta}\begin{pmatrix}b&2d\\-2a&-b\end{pmatrix}\right)$$
has eigenvalues $\omega,\omega^2$ and fixes $H$. It preserves $q$ and the harmonic cubic $f_3$, and hence the whole curve. Its inverse corresponds to changing the sign of the square root. In particular the subgroup and quotient descend over $\mathbb Q$, and its generator is rational exactly when $-3/\Delta$ is a rational square.

Put $r=J(f_3)/3$. Diagonalizing on $L^3,N^3$ gives $r^2-\Delta f_3^2=b_0q^3$. Both eigen-coefficients of $f_3$ are nonzero; otherwise the source would be singular at one of the two contact points. Thus $b_0\ne0$. The invariant functions and their target are
$$t=q/H^2,\qquad w=r/H^3,\qquad w^2=\Delta(ct^2+d_0t+e)^2+b_0t^3.$$
The first function has pole order three at each of the contact points. Indeed $H$ vanishes twice there and $q$ vanishes simply, since $f_3$ does not vanish there. Hence $[\mathbb Q(C):\mathbb Q(t)]=6$. Over the algebraic closure the invariants on $H=1$ are generated by $LN,L^3,N^3$: an invariant monomial has the difference of its exponents divisible by three. The two cubics $f_3,r$ span $L^3,N^3$, and $f_3$ is a polynomial in $t$ on the curve. Thus $t,w$ generate the geometric invariant field, the map has degree three and the residual degree over $t$ is two. Its genus is one by Riemann–Hurwitz. The program independently verifies squarefreeness and substitutes the recovered functions in the source equation. It computes $L(3D)$ and $L(6D)$ at the contact divisor $D$ and checks that both reconstructed generators lie in their stated spaces.

On the [Fermat input](fermat.json), the calculation reconstructs all four rational cyclic cubic maps from the equation, with target invariant $287496$, agreeing with Part XXVIII, §§128 and 132. The [mixed input](nonsplit.json) has one rational bitangent and one such class. Its contact points are defined over $\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-2})$ and the deck transformations over $\mathbb Q(\sqrt6)$. A second control has two rational contacts and deck field $\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-3})$. Changing coordinates on that control tests all three dual charts. The negative controls include a smooth deformation whose ordinary bitangent and first normal cubic persist while an added $H^3A$ term destroys its cyclic quotient, a curve with two hyperflex lines, and a curve with no rational bitangents.
